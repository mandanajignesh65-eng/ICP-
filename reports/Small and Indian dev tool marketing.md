# How Small Developer Tools Get Famous

Small developer tools almost never get famous through ads or Instagram. They get famous by giving developers something free and useful, often open source. Then they launch it where developers already are: GitHub, Hacker News (HN), Reddit and X. Indian tools like Hoppscotch, Bruno, Requestly and ToolJet grew this way and spent almost nothing on marketing. Indian channels (LinkedIn, Peerlist, r/developersIndia, college hackathons) help with trust, hiring and reaching students. On their own, they rarely bring paying users. Reels from Indian coding creators are cheap (about ₹16,000–35,000 each), but no dev tool has published signups from them. Money comes slowly. Expect only 1–5% of free users to pay, and sell to teams more than to students. For a small Indian tool, the best mix is: one free open-source hook, one big global launch, steady founder posts, cheap student hackathon prizes, and INR prices with UPI.

*Note: We use about ₹88 = $1. Numbers marked "~" are approximate. Many are self-reported by founders. Most sources could only be read as search summaries, because page fetching was blocked. Please check key numbers before you reuse them.*

## Which channels work: a scorecard

**In short:** Free, developer-only channels (GitHub, HN, store search, SEO) bring the most users. Paid social is cheap in India, but it is unproven for signups.

| Channel | Cost (INR) | Effort | What it gives you | Evidence |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Open source on GitHub | ₹0 | High | Stars, contributors, trust | **Strong** (Hoppscotch, ToolJet, Plane) |

| Product Hunt | ₹0 | Medium | ~2,500 visitors for a #1–#2 finish ([source](https://github.com/fmerian/awesome-product-hunt/blob/main/product-hunt-launch-guide.md)) | Medium |
| Chrome / VS Code store search | ₹0 | Low, compounding | Steady users over time (Requestly) | Medium–strong |

## Instagram, LinkedIn and short video: real numbers

**In short:** Brand accounts of small dev tools stay tiny. Reach comes from founders' personal profiles and from paying many creators.

### Instagram

- **Brand accounts are small.** Screen Studio has ~693 Instagram followers ([source](https://www.instagram.com/screenstudioapp/)). Jam.dev has ~1,666 TikTok followers ([source](https://www.tiktok.com/@jamdotdev)).
- **Indian coding creators you can hire** (Collabstr prices):

| @himmat.tech.ai | Patiala | not shown | Post $50 (~₹4,400) ([source](https://collabstr.com/himmatai)) |

- **Big education accounts:** CodeWithHarry has ~794K Instagram followers and ~10M YouTube subscribers (Oct 2026) ([source](https://creatordb.app/creatorstats/codewithharry/)). @shradhakhapra (Apna College) has ~789K ([source](https://www.instagram.com/shradhakhapra/)). Their audience is mostly students preparing for placements.
- **Coding meme pages:** @_codehub_ ~124K, @codingkovalu ~57K. Indian meme pages with 10K–50K followers charge ₹400–1,000 per post ([source](https://instaboostpanel.com/blog/instagram-shoutout-price-india/)). Never pay fully in advance.
- **Comment-to-DM is the standard trick.** The reel says "comment TOOLS". A bot then DMs the link. Ask creators to put your UTM link in their DM flow.

### LinkedIn

- **The founder's profile matters, not the company page.** CodeAnt's founder has ~42K followers. The company page has only ~1K–2.2K ([source](https://www.linkedin.com/in/amartya-jha/)).
- Milestone posts ("we hit 52,000 GitHub stars") build trust but get modest engagement, about 11–12 comments ([source](https://www.linkedin.com/posts/liyasthomas_hoppscotch-just-passed-52000-stars-on-github-activity-7077690930786414592-9vHN)).
- Carousels (PDF posts) get the most engagement: 2.44% vs 1.53% for text ([source](https://www.tryordinal.com/blog/carousel-posts)). Personal profiles get ~8x the engagement of company pages ([source](https://contentin.io/blog/linkedin-content-statistics/)).

### Short video

- **Comedy sketches with recurring characters:** tl;dv posted ~800 TikToks. The median video got ~25K views. It took ~6 months before follower growth took off. Social later brought up to a third of signups ([source](https://www.lennysnewsletter.com/p/60-creative-growth-ideas)).
- **Many paid creators:** Composio paid 100+ creators at $1 per 1,000 views (~₹88). They posted 4,000+ videos and got ~200M views in ~3 months (self-reported) ([source](https://www.linkedin.com/in/victorg8182/)). Its audience was young and mostly in the US.
- **Going rates:** tl;dv pays $100 per 20K views, capped at $700 per post. Whop "Content Rewards" averages ~$1.25 per 1,000 views plus a 10% fee ([source](https://shopiator.com/blog/whop-content-reward-campaigns)).

## Community tactics that worked

**In short:** Give students and contributors a reason to use your tool (prizes, badges, credit, jobs). Then answer them fast.

| Tactic | Who | Result | Cost |
|---|---|---|---|
| Hacktoberfest + "good first issue" labels | ToolJet | 72 new contributors in its first Hacktoberfest ([source](https://blog.tooljet.com/tooljets-first-hacktoberfest-it-turned-out-to-be-great/)) | ₹0 |
| Digital badges for contributors | Keploy | Stars up 100% in a quarter, issues up 300% (vendor case) ([source](https://dev.to/vaunt/keploy-leverages-vaunt-to-celebrate-developer-contributions-143i)) | Low |
| Hire from contributors | Keploy | 3 early contributors became the founding engineers ([source](https://wp.keploy.io/?p=2571)) | ₹0 |
| Big community hackathon | WeMakeDevs (sponsors Cerebras, Meta, Docker) | 5,500+ participants, 7,000+ API signups ([source](https://archive.wemakedevs.org/hackathons/futurestack25)) | Not public |
| "Best use of X" prize | Composio at Hack4Bengal | Students build with the tool ([source](https://dev.hackquest.io/en/hackathons/Hack4Bengal)) | ~$100 prize |
| Flat grant to every student hackathon | Polygon on Devfolio | Became "a known brand among Indian student developers" ([source](https://devfolio.co/blog/turning-up-heat-for-winter-winds-at-devfolio/)) | ₹15,000 per event |
| Slack community linked from launch posts | SigNoz | 1,800 → 6,000+ members ([source](https://www.ycombinator.com/companies/signoz/jobs)) | ₹0 |
| Small paid bounties (Algora) | Coolify | 27 bounties of $15–100 each, $1,402 total ([source](https://algora.io/coollabsio/bounties/community)) | ~₹1.2 lakh total |
| Open numbers and yearly recaps | Coolify, Bruno | Users trust the tool will survive | ₹0 |

ToolJet's main lesson: "product-community fit" matters, so reply to issues and PRs fast ([source](https://blog.tooljet.com/zero-to-10-000-stargazers-on-github/)). Note that student hackathons bring stars and contributors, not buyers.

## What failed: likes but no money

**In short:** Launch-day crowds are mostly other makers, not buyers. Stars and views do not pay bills.

## How small tools make money from Indian developers

**In short:** Keep individuals on a cheap INR plan with UPI. Make real money from teams and companies.

- **Expect low conversion.** About 1–5% of free users pay. snappify had ~40 payers from 1,000 accounts (~4%) ([source](https://www.indiehackers.com/product/snappify/1000-mrr--NJ4NwCuCqLCcXGHND_W)). A survey of 200 products found a median of 8% ([source](https://chartmogul.com/reports/saas-conversion-report/)).
- **Dollar prices hurt.** A "$20" tool costs an Indian developer ~₹2,000 per month after GST and forex ([source](https://www.indiasbestaitools.com/pricing-in-inr/)). Many Indian debit cards fail on foreign recurring payments.
- **Use local pricing.** Typical PPP discounts for India are 60–75% ([source](https://fungies.io/ppp-pricing-saas-boost-global-revenue-2026)). One contrast: even Cursor launched a ₹649/month India plan with UPI in 2026 ([source](https://cursor.com/blog/cursor-start-india)).
- **Use Razorpay or Cashfree for UPI Autopay.** Stripe India is invite-only and has no UPI ([source](https://rohitraj.tech/notes/razorpay-vs-stripe-india-mvp-2026)).
- **Charge teams.** Hoppscotch charges $6 per user per month for teams. Enterprise features (SSO, audit logs) are the real money gate ([source](https://www.everydev.ai/developers/hoppscotch)). CodeAnt won Indian companies like Tata 1mg and Cipla first.
- **A long free period can work.** Locofy stayed free for ~3.5 years. Then it sold 1,000+ subscriptions in 2 weeks after launching paid plans (company-reported) ([source](https://businessmodelcanvastemplate.com/blogs/brief-history/locofy-brief-history)).

## A 90-day plan for a small Indian developer productivity tool

**In short:** First build one free hook. Then do one big launch. Then add paid reach only where you can track signups.

**Budget tiers (for 90 days)**

| Tier | Total | What you add |
|---|---|---|
| Starter | ₹0–25,000 | Open source, HN/PH/Reddit/Peerlist, founder posts, a comment-to-DM tool, 1 student hackathon grant (₹15,000) |
| Growth | ~₹1–1.5 lakh | Starter + 3 coding-creator reels (₹16,000–35,000 each) + 2 more hackathon grants + a few Algora bounties |
| Push | ~₹4–5 lakh | Growth + a pay-per-view creator pool (₹1.5 lakh ≈ 8–17 lakh views at $1–2 per 1,000) + 1 hackathon track with a creator-led community (ask for price) + 1 newsletter test |

**Days 1–30: get ready**
- Pick one clear line: "Open-source / offline / faster alternative to [tool developers dislike]".
- Open-source the core or a small useful side tool. Add "good first issue" labels.
- Set up Razorpay with UPI Autopay, an INR price (~₹300–700 per month for individuals) and a team plan.
- Add "How did you hear about us?" to signup. Put UTM links on every channel.
- Founder posts 3 times a week on LinkedIn and daily on X. Write 2 dev.to or Hashnode posts about the problem, not the product.
- Share the repo privately with ~50 friendly developers first, as Plane did.

**Days 31–60: launch**
- Launch day: Product Hunt in the morning, Show HN a few hours later. The founder answers every comment.
- In the same week, post on Peerlist and in r/developersIndia "Showcase Sunday" (second Sunday of the month). Ask for feedback, not upvotes.
- Afterwards, share a screenshot of any ranking on LinkedIn.
- Growth/Push: book creator reels. Ask for a "comment TOOL" DM flow with your link.

**Days 61–90: community and money**
- Sponsor "Best use of [tool]" prizes at 1–3 GDG on Campus or Devfolio hackathons (~₹15,000 each).
- In October, join Hacktoberfest. Thank contributors in public.
- Open a Discord or Slack. Reply within one day.
- Email active free users with a team offer.
- Keep only the channels where signups (not likes) show up in your survey.

## Conclusion

Fame is cheap; customers are not. The Indian winners solved a real pain for working developers and used global developer channels. Treat Indian creators and channels as tests to measure, not the main engine. Nobody has published signup data from Instagram, LinkedIn or Indian creators, so a tool that tracks this well will know more than the market.
