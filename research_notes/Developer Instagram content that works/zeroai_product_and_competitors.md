## 1. What is ZeroAI (zeroai.co.in): product, features, pricing, platform, company, founders, launch status

### Takeaway
ZeroAI, now branded "Zero" in its Chrome Web Store title, is a **free Chrome extension** for developers. It pulls tasks and commitments out of Slack, Jira/Linear, email and meetings, and books "focus time" or "Deep Work" blocks for them on the user's Google Calendar. It also takes meeting notes and runs an end-of-day close-out. Its current headline is **"Zero: You Code. It Handles Slack, Meetings & Calendar."** It belongs to the portfolio of **Candela Labs Private Limited**, the Singapore-registered company behind NewsBytes and DevBytes, whose named founders are Shikha Chaudhry, Sumedh Chaudhry and Romesh Khaddar. I could not confirm that any of them are ZeroAI's founders. I found no funding, Product Hunt launch or press coverage.

### Inferences
- The core promise for content is **"you code, Zero handles the coordination layer."** That layer is Slack asks, Jira/Linear tickets, meetings and calendar Tetris. It is a calendar-plus-task-capture product, not a distraction blocker and not an IDE tool. The strongest hooks match its real features: "that Slack 'can you quickly…' just became a calendar block," "the meeting notes write themselves," and a "where did your focus time go?" two-week audit.
- The "scans the next two weeks and shows where focus time went" onboarding step is a natural reel or carousel format: a before/after calendar screenshot and a "your week, audited" reveal.
- Because it is free and read-only to start, low-risk CTAs fit: "Add to Chrome, free, read-only calendar."
- Candela Labs also runs **DevBytes**, a developer news app. If marketing can coordinate with it, DevBytes is an owned distribution channel to Indian developers. This is an inference only.
- The rating looks strong (4.8–4.96) on a small base (65–138 ratings), and installs are in the thousands. It is an early-stage product, so "trusted by 7,000+ developers" is the biggest number it can honestly use.

## 3. Competitors and adjacent tools: how they message focus, deep work and developer productivity, and their social content

### Takeaway
ZeroAI's closest functional competitors are **AI calendar and focus-time schedulers**: Reclaim.ai, Motion, Sunsama and, until March 2026, Clockwise. Around them sit time trackers (Rize), flow and blocker apps (Centered, Serene, Freedom, Cold Turkey) and AI context tools (Pieces, Slackbot/Slack AI). The category's copy clusters around "defend/protect focus time," "calm," "flow state," "know where your time goes," and quantified hours-saved claims. **Clockwise's shutdown on March 27, 2026 left a gap**, and Reclaim is actively courting Clockwise's former users. I found no evidence of any of these tools getting standout Instagram Reel traction.

## 4. Developer pain points and credible statistics (interruptions, meetings, Slack pings, context switching, searching)

### Takeaway
The best-sourced, citable numbers come from three sources. **Atlassian's State of DevEx 2025**: 50% of developers lose 10+ hours a week to organizational inefficiencies, and 90% lose 6+. **Stack Overflow 2024**: 61% spend 30+ minutes a day searching for answers. **Microsoft Work Trend Index 2025 ("infinite workday")**: heavily contacted users get about 275 interruptions, roughly one every 2 minutes. Gloria Mark's "23 minutes 15 seconds" is widely cited, but its primary sourcing is weak and it is often misquoted.

### Cited Findings

**Atlassian State of Developer Experience 2025** (VERIFIED, first-party blog)
- 50% of developers report losing **10+ hours per week**, and 90% lose **6+ hours**, "largely due to organizational inefficiencies." — [Atlassian blog](https://www.atlassian.com/blog/developer/developer-experience-report-2025)
- The share losing 10+ hours rose from 33% in the previous year's report. — [Atlassian Team '25 Europe session](https://events.atlassian.com/teameurope/session/3225389/state-of-devex-2025-navigating-the-ai-revolution-and-developer-experience)
- 99% of developers report time savings from AI, and 68% save 10+ hours a week. The savings are offset by friction such as "searching for services, documents, APIs." — [Atlassian blog](https://www.atlassian.com/blog/developer/developer-experience-report-2025)
- The survey covered 3,500 developers and managers across six countries. Diginomica puts the cost at about $7.9M a year for a 500-developer company; that is Diginomica's restatement. — [Diginomica](https://diginomica.com/node/29030); [Atlassian report PDF](https://dam-cdn.atl.orangelogic.com/AssetLink/5yt05dl5q8s1xljrs8747h8x6240c32p.pdf)
- Secondary coverage frames it as "AI productivity gains are being offset by organizational bottlenecks." — [LeadDev](https://leaddev.com/velocity/ai-productivity-gains-are-being-offset-by-organizational-bottlenecks)

**Stack Overflow Developer Survey** (VERIFIED, first-party)
- 2024: **61%** of respondents spend 30+ minutes a day searching for answers or solutions, and about 1 in 4 spend 60+ minutes. The question counts searching, asking colleagues and *waiting for a response*. — [SO 2024 survey: professional developers](https://survey.stackoverflow.co/2024/professional-developers); [Stack Overflow insights](https://stackoverflow.co/teams/resources/your-developers-deserve-better-insights-from-the-2024-developer-survey)
- 2022: about 62–63% spend 30+ minutes a day, and 25% spend more than an hour. Sources vary slightly. — [Stack Overflow blog 2022](https://stackoverflow.blog/2022/06/22/asked-and-answered-the-results-for-the-2022-developer-survey-are-here/)

**Microsoft Work Trend Index 2025, "Breaking down the infinite workday"** (REPORTED via press; primary report not opened)
- Based on Microsoft 365 telemetry plus a survey of 31,000 workers in 31 markets. — [Workplace Insight](https://workplaceinsight.net/microsoft-research-lays-bare-the-rise-of-the-infinite-workday/)
- Workers are interrupted about **every 2 minutes** during the core workday, or **275 times a day**. Axios says a ping "every 1.75 minutes." **Caveat:** these figures are for the **top 20% most-pinged users**, and the 275 may span 24 hours. — [Axios](https://www.axios.com/2025/06/17/microsoft-remote-work-meetings); [Silicon Canals analysis](https://siliconcanals.com/m-by-one-2025-microsoft-tally-a-worker-is-interrupted-every-two-minutes-of-the-working-day-around-275-times-as-after-hours-pings-and-meetings-past-eight-in-the-evening-kee/)
- Average of 117 emails and 153 Teams messages per workday. Ad hoc (unscheduled) calls make up about 57–60% of meetings; outlets differ on the figure. — [ACS Information Age](https://ia.acs.org.au/article/2025/workday-now-dominated-by--focus-sapping--meetings.html); [Business Today](https://www.businesstoday.in/amp/technology/news/story/microsoft-report-warns-of-the-infinite-workday-calls-for-radical-ai-led-reinvention-480791-2025-06-18)

**Gloria Mark (UC Irvine), recovery after interruption** (REPORTED, with caveats)
- In a 2006 Gallup interview, Mark said researchers shadowed 36 workers (including software developers) for three days. The average time to return to interrupted work was **23 minutes 15 seconds**, with about two other tasks done in between. The interval *includes* handling the interruption and doing other work, so it is not 23 minutes of "refocusing." — [Gallup Business Journal](https://news.gallup.com/businessjournal/23146/Too-Many-Interruptions-Work.aspx)
- A 2023 blog investigation could not find a primary printed paper with the 23:15 figure. It appears mainly in interviews, including the WSJ. — [Silicon Canals](https://siliconcanals.com/t-work-interruptions-23-minutes-275-pings-organisational-focus/); [Fast Company](https://www.fastcompany.com/944128/worker-interrupted-cost-task-switching)

### Inferences
- **Safest stats for Instagram graphics, with the citation line for each:**
  1. "Half of developers lose 10+ hours a week to org friction" (Atlassian, State of DevEx 2025).
  2. "61% of devs spend 30+ min a day just searching or waiting for answers" (Stack Overflow Developer Survey 2024).

  4. "~23 minutes to get back to an interrupted task" (Gloria Mark, UC Irvine, via Gallup 2006). Phrase it as "to get back to," not "to refocus."

Searching for docs or APIs (Atlassian, Stack Overflow) is a pain point ZeroAI does **not** demonstrably solve.
