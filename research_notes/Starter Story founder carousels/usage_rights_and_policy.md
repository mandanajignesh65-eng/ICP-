# Usage rights and policy: can ZeroAI use Starter Story founder content in Instagram carousels?

> General information gathered on 15 Oct 2026, not legal advice. ZeroAI is a small Indian developer-tool company. It should get advice from a lawyer licensed in India (and in the US if it targets US audiences) before running paid ads that use real people.
>
> Research method note: starterstory.com/terms and /copyright-dmca were read in full with a direct download (WebFetch failed on DNS, but curl worked). The HubSpot Website Terms of Use (linked from Starter Story's footer as "Website Terms of Use") were also read in full. Instagram help pages, PetaPixel and TechCrunch blocked direct fetching, so the Instagram findings rely on search-result summaries of TechCrunch, Tubefilter and PetaPixel.

## 1. Starter Story's terms, and the HubSpot terms that now apply

### Takeaway
Starter Story's own terms say its data may be republished **only for non-commercial purposes ("e.g. not a company/startup")** and require a link back. They also say copyright in the site belongs to Starter Story. The HubSpot Website Terms of Use, now linked from the Starter Story footer, allow use of site content **only for "non-commercial, personal purposes"** and ban commercial exploitation. A brand account promoting its product is commercial use under both sets of terms. There is no public content-licensing or permission process. The only way to use the content under these terms is to get written permission.

### Cited Findings
- **Scope:** Starter Story's terms "apply to Starter Story and any other of Starter Story's sites, apps, communities on any platform." The page states "Starter Story is part of HubSpot." — [Starter Story Terms & Conditions](https://www.starterstory.com/terms)
- **Non-commercial only:** The TL;DR section says: "You can only re-publish our data if it's for non-commercial objectives (e.g. not a company/startup)." — [Starter Story Terms](https://www.starterstory.com/terms)
- **Link-back requirement:** The TL;DR says: "If you use one of our APIs or data, you're required to link back to us at every page or in every app screen you use the data on." The official terms repeat it: "You agree to link back with a web hyperlink or in-app hyperlink to our site on the page or app screen where you use the data from our APIs or site." — [Starter Story Terms](https://www.starterstory.com/terms)
- **Copyright ownership:** "Copyright in Starter Story's websites, apps, communities and its properties rests with Starter Story unless otherwise stated." — [Starter Story Terms](https://www.starterstory.com/terms)
- **Accuracy disclaimer:** The data is "crowdsourced and [has] user generated content, so don't rely on the data", and users "should verify critical information." This matters for revenue claims in ads. — [Starter Story Terms](https://www.starterstory.com/terms)
- **Founder contributions:** When founders contribute content, they grant Starter Story "a perpetual license to broadcast, display, distribute, sell and produce derivatives from your contributions." The licence goes to Starter Story only, not to third parties such as ZeroAI. — [Starter Story Terms](https://www.starterstory.com/terms)
- **Governing law:** Starter Story's legacy terms are governed by "the laws of Amsterdam, The Netherlands." — [Starter Story Terms](https://www.starterstory.com/terms)
- **Footer link to HubSpot terms:** The Starter Story footer's "Website Terms of Use" link points to legal.hubspot.com/website-terms-of-use. Its signup forms say "HubSpot uses your information to contact you…" — [Starter Story Terms page HTML](https://www.starterstory.com/terms)
- **HubSpot terms coverage:** The HubSpot terms (Last Modified: April 14, 2026) apply to "HubSpot.com, and any website where we display these terms (each a 'Site')." — [HubSpot Website Terms of Use](https://legal.hubspot.com/website-terms-of-use)
- **HubSpot permitted use:** Content ("information, writings, images and/or other works") may be used "solely for your non-commercial, personal purposes and/or to learn about HubSpot." — [HubSpot Website Terms of Use](https://legal.hubspot.com/website-terms-of-use)
- **HubSpot prohibited use:** Users may not "copy, modify, create a derivative work from…" a Site, or "sell, assign, sublicense, distribute, commercially exploit… the Content." Also: "the Content may not be copied, reproduced, modified, published, uploaded, posted, transmitted… or distributed in any way… [or used to] create derivative works." HubSpot "owns and retains all of its copyrights in the Content." — [HubSpot Website Terms of Use](https://legal.hubspot.com/website-terms-of-use)
- **HubSpot governing law:** Disputes are governed by Massachusetts law. — [HubSpot Website Terms of Use](https://legal.hubspot.com/website-terms-of-use)
- **Enforcement process:** Starter Story has a DMCA policy with a designated agent (Trust & Safety Team, P.O. Box 3003, Jackson, WY 83001). It forwards complaints to Lumen. — [Starter Story Copyright & DMCA Policy](https://www.starterstory.com/copyright-dmca)
- **Acquisition:** HubSpot acquired Starter Story in late February 2026. It operates inside HubSpot Media alongside The Hustle, My First Million and Trends. Founder Pat Walls joined HubSpot Media. — [Adweek](https://www.adweek.com/media/hubspot-media-acquires-starter-story-youtube/); [MarTech](https://martech.org/hubspot-supercharges-its-media-engine-by-buying-starter-story/); [ContentGrip](https://www.contentgrip.com/hubspot-acquires-starter-story-media/)

### Inferences
- Under both the Starter Story terms and the HubSpot terms, a ZeroAI marketing carousel is commercial use, so "Source: Starter Story" does **not** make it compliant. A credit meets the link-back requirement but not the non-commercial limit.
- HubSpot Media is now a large, well-resourced owner that runs its own business-founder content. It is more likely than an independent creator to notice and enforce. The most likely enforcement routes are an Instagram IP report or a DMCA-style takedown, rather than a lawsuit against a small Indian company. Repeated reports still put the account at risk (see section 4).
- Terms of use bind people who use the site. Whether they bind ZeroAI depends on contract-formation rules (browsewrap). Copyright in Starter Story's own text, photos and video frames applies whether or not ZeroAI agreed to anything.
- Neither set of terms offers a self-serve licence. The practical route is to email Starter Story or HubSpot Media for permission, or to go straight to the founder for their own facts, quotes and photos.

### Gaps
- No public Starter Story or HubSpot Media content-licensing, press-kit or "permissions" page was found. I could not confirm whether HubSpot has formally replaced Starter Story's legacy terms or whether both apply side by side. The terms page shows both the old Starter Story text and a footer link to the HubSpot terms.
- No evidence was found of Starter Story or HubSpot taking enforcement action against social carousel creators.

## 2. Copyright: facts, short quotes and photos

### Takeaway
Bare facts such as a founder's name, monthly revenue figure or tech stack are **not** protected by copyright. Starter Story's wording, article layout, video frames and photos **are** protected. Short quotes with attribution are defensible in editorial commentary but weak in an advertisement, and India's fair-dealing defence is narrower than US fair use. Cutting a founder out of a Starter Story photo or video frame and pasting them into an ad is the highest copyright risk in the whole plan.

### Cited Findings
- **Facts (US):** In *Feist v. Rural* (1991), the Supreme Court held that facts lack authorship and so cannot be copyrighted. A compilation is protected only for its original selection or arrangement, and "the copyright does not extend to facts contained in the compilation." It also rejected the "sweat of the brow" theory. — [Wikipedia: Feist v. Rural](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Feist_Publications,_Inc._v._Rural_Telephone_Service_Co.); [Chanrobles syllabus](https://chanrobles.com/usa/us_supremecourt/499/340/)
- **Names and short phrases:** The US Copyright Office lists "Names, titles, and short phrases" and "Ideas, methods, and systems" among material not protected by copyright. — [US Copyright Office Circular 33](https://www.copyright.gov/circs/circ33.pdf)
- **US fair use factor 1:** Courts weigh whether a use "is of a commercial nature or is for nonprofit educational purposes"; "transformative" uses that "add something new, with a further purpose or different character, and do not substitute for the original" are more likely to be fair. — [US Copyright Office Fair Use Index](https://www.copyright.gov/fair-use/)
- **US fair use factor 2:** Factual works such as "a technical article or news item" support fair use more than creative works. — [US Copyright Office Fair Use Index](https://www.copyright.gov/fair-use/)
- **US fair use factor 3:** Using a large portion, or the "heart," of a work weighs against fair use. — [US Copyright Office Fair Use Index](https://www.copyright.gov/fair-use/)
- **Photos (US):** In *Warhol v. Goldsmith* (2023), the Supreme Court held 7–2 that factor one weighed against the commercial licensing of a work based on a photographer's portrait. The new use (illustrating a magazine story about the subject) shared the original photo's commercial purpose. New expression "is not, without more, dispositive." Goldsmith, the photographer, owned the photo's copyright, not Prince, the subject. — [Cornell LII opinion](https://www.law.cornell.edu/supremecourt/text/21-869); [Copyright Office summary](https://www.copyright.gov/fair-use/summaries/Andy-Warhol-Found-for-the-Visual-Arts-Inc-v-Goldsmith-143-S-Ct-1258-2023.pdf); [Paul Weiss memo](https://www.paulweiss.com/insights/client-memos/supreme-court-holds-that-andy-warhol-foundation-s-licensing-of-prince-portrait-is-not-fair-use)
- **India, s.52(1)(a):** Fair dealing is allowed only for listed purposes: private or personal use including research, criticism or review, and reporting current events and current affairs. Indian law requires the use to fit one of these purposes, unlike open-ended US fair use. — [Cyril Amarchand Mangaldas blog (June 2025)](https://corporate.cyrilamarchandblogs.com/2025/06/fair-dealing-in-the-digital-age-navigating-copyright-for-news-and-online-content-in-india/); [BananaIP](https://bananaip.com/?p=39294)
- **India, fairness test:** Indian courts, including the Delhi High Court, have read fairness through factors similar to the US four-factor test, and whether a use is commercial is relevant. *Civic Chandran v. Ammini Amma* (Kerala HC) protected quotations used for criticism. Extensive copying is harder to defend. — [Cyril Amarchand](https://corporate.cyrilamarchandblogs.com/2025/06/fair-dealing-in-the-digital-age-navigating-copyright-for-news-and-online-content-in-india/); [SpicyIP on Aaj Tak v. Newslaundry](https://spicyip.com/2021/11/aaj-tak-v-newslaundry-assessing-the-fair-dealing-question.html); [Lawful Legal on Civic Chandran](https://lawfullegal.in/civic-chandran-v-ammini-amma-manu-ke-0675-1996-fair-dealing-free-speech-and-copyright/)
- **Instagram on credit:** Instagram's help centre says infringement can happen even if you credit the owner or do not profit from the content. — search summary of [Instagram Help Center (copyright)](https://help.Instagram.com/354736791367645)

### Inferences
- **Lowest risk:** restating facts in ZeroAI's own words and design, for example "Founder X built a $10K/mo tool with Next.js + Supabase." Feist and Circular 33 support this. Starter Story's terms still restrict commercial republishing of "our data," so the risk is contract and relationship risk, not copyright.
- **Medium risk:** short quotes copied word for word from a Starter Story interview. The founder's interview answers may be the founder's own expression, licensed to Starter Story, and Starter Story owns the article. In India, an ad does not fit neatly into "criticism or review" or "reporting current events," so fair dealing is a weak defence. In the US, a commercial, non-commentary use weakens fair use under factor 1, particularly after *Warhol*.
- **Highest risk:** a founder photo or video frame cut out and placed on a doodle background. Copyright in a photo usually belongs to the photographer or their employer: Starter Story or HubSpot for frames from its own videos, or a freelance or the founder's own photographer for submitted headshots. The founder usually does not own it unless they took it or commissioned it under an assignment. Cutting out the subject and adding doodles is still a derivative use of the protected image. After *Warhol*, styling alone is unlikely to make an ad that features the same person transformative. In India, an advertisement is not one of the s.52(1)(a) purposes.
- Under India's Copyright Act, the photographer is generally the author and first owner unless an employment or commissioning arrangement provides otherwise. That rule comes from my general knowledge of ss.2(d) and 17. I did not verify it against the statute text in this session (see Gaps).

### Gaps
- I did not fetch the primary text of Indian Copyright Act s.52 or ss.2(d) and 17 from indiacode.nic.in. The findings above rely on secondary commentary.
- I found no Indian case applying fair dealing to quotes used in commercial advertising.

## 3. Personality rights and the right of publicity: real name and face in an ad

### Takeaway
Using a real founder's **name and face to promote ZeroAI**, without consent, is the most serious legal exposure, more than copyright. US state law (for example California Civil Code §3344) prohibits knowing use of a person's name or photo in advertising without consent. Delhi High Court orders from 2023–2025 have protected not only film stars but also entrepreneurs and creators such as Ankur Warikoo and Dr Devi Shetty against unauthorised commercial use of their persona. A slide flow of "this founder makes $10K/mo… use ZeroAI for deep work" risks implying that the founder endorses or uses ZeroAI.

### Cited Findings
- **California §3344:** Covers knowing use of another's "name, voice, signature, photograph, or likeness" in advertising, selling or promoting products or services without prior consent. Subdivision (d) exempts uses "in connection with any news, public affairs, or sports broadcast or account." The misappropriation must be "directly connected" to the commercial purpose. — [The Fashion Law: Right of Publicity](https://www.thefashionlaw.com/resource-center/right-of-publicity/); [Vondran Legal](https://www.vondranlegal.com/first-amendment-and-public-affairs-defenses-under-california-right-of-publicity-law)
- **California SB 683:** Signed 10 Oct 2025. It adds injunctive relief to §3344 and extends "voice" and "likeness" to digital replicas. — [Hunton Andrews Kurth](https://www.hunton.com/privacy-and-information-security-law/california-adds-injunctive-relief-to-its-right-of-publicity-statute-and-extends-liability-to-digital-replicas)
- ***Eastwood v. Superior Court*:** A cover and ads that used Clint Eastwood's name and likeness to sell a publication were treated as commercial exploitation. The news exemption does not protect knowingly false implications. — [LexPlug case brief](https://www.lexplug.com/casebrief/eastwood_v_superior_court_65bb48ad3b1ee3f39d4ef67a)
- ***Ankur Warikoo v. John Doe* (Delhi HC, 26 May 2025, 2025 SCC OnLine Del 3727):** Warikoo is an entrepreneur, finance educator and creator with about 15.1M followers. Justice Amit Bansal granted an ex parte ad interim injunction against misuse of his name, image, voice and likeness, including AI deepfakes promoting stock-tip WhatsApp groups. Platforms were told to remove content within 36 hours. — [WIPO Lex case summary](https://www.wipo.int/wipolex/en/text/596125); [LiveLaw](https://www.livelaw.in/amp/high-court/delhi-high-court/delhi-high-court-ankur-warikoo-deepfake-videos-restrained-293539); [SCC Online blog](https://www.scconline.com/blog/post/2025/05/29/delhi-high-court-ankur-warikoo-john-doe-injunction-deepfake-ai-misuse-legal-news/)
- **Other Delhi HC orders:** Aishwarya Rai Bachchan (9 Sept 2025, Justice Tejas Karia, merchandise using her likeness), Abhishek Bachchan (Sept 2025), Jaya Bachchan (Nov 2025) and R. Madhavan (Dec 2025). The court treated unconsented commercial use as prima facie infringement and ordered takedowns. Most are interim orders. — [LiveLaw (Abhishek Bachchan)](https://www.livelaw.in/amp/high-court/delhi-high-court/delhi-high-court-protects-actor-abhishek-bachchans-personality-rights-303629); [Hollywood Reporter India (Aishwarya Rai)](https://www.hollywoodreporterindia.com/features/insight/delhi-hc-grants-aishwarya-rai-protection-from-misuse-of-name-and-image); [Storyboard18 (Jaya Bachchan)](https://www.storyboard18.com/brand-makers/delhi-hc-backs-jaya-bachchan-in-fight-against-ai-misuse-and-fake-merch-protects-personality-rights-83939.htm); [Hogan Lovells tracker (Madhavan)](https://digital-client-solutions.hoganlovells.com/resources/ai-litigation-case-law-tracker/case/ranganathan-madhavan-v-g-filmz-studioz/decision/43)
- **Doctrinal criticism:** SpicyIP argues that the Delhi HC orders (Sadhguru, Warikoo) "presume that recognisability of a person automatically gives rise to infringement." In its view, personality rights are "quietly but significantly expanding." It names *Dr. Devi Prasad Shetty v. Medicine Me* (identity misuse affecting public health) and the *Jackie Shroff* case. In *Jackie Shroff*, the court refused blanket injunctions and protected memes, parody and non-derogatory commentary under Article 19(1)(a). — [SpicyIP, June 2025](https://spicyip.com/2025/06/delhi-hcs-orders-in-sadhguru-ankur-warikoo-cases-what-indian-courts-are-getting-wrong-about-personality-rights.html)

### Inferences
- **Indian courts:** Indian courts no longer limit personality rights to film celebrities. Entrepreneurs and creators with a recognisable public persona (Warikoo, Shetty) have obtained relief. A typical Starter Story founder is a small indie maker and not a celebrity, so a claim would be weaker. Still, the recognisability-equals-infringement trend makes using their face in an ad without consent risky. Courts also act fast and ex parte, and order platform takedowns.
- **Implied endorsement:** A carousel that shows Founder X's photo and revenue, then moves to "use ZeroAI for deep work," invites viewers to think Founder X uses or recommends ZeroAI. That is the misleading, commercial-advantage use that §3344 and the Delhi HC orders target. In the US, this would also likely support a Lanham Act §43(a) false-endorsement claim. That is my inference; I did not search for a source in this session. The "news/public affairs" exemption is unlikely to cover a product ad.
- **Reducing the risk:** Making the content genuinely editorial (a lesson about the founder's workflow) and visually separating the CTA reduces the risk but does not remove it. The account itself is a brand account selling a product.
- **Foreign founders:** Most Starter Story founders are in the US or elsewhere. A US founder suing an Indian startup is unlikely in practice. The realistic consequences are a cease-and-desist, a public call-out on X or Instagram, an Instagram report and takedown, and damage to ZeroAI's reputation in the indie-hacker community, which is ZeroAI's target market.

- I found no Indian case involving a non-celebrity small founder whose face was used in a startup's social ad.

## 4. Instagram's policies: IP rules, originality and reach penalties

### Takeaway
Instagram's terms prohibit posting content that infringes others' IP, and crediting the source does not cure infringement. Repeat infringement can get the account disabled. Since **30 April 2026**, Instagram's originality rules cover **photos and carousels**, not only Reels. An account where **most** of the last ~30 days of posts are other people's content becomes ineligible for recommendations. "Meaningfully transformed" content, such as original graphics with new information or added context, can still count as original.

### Cited Findings
- **IP rule:** You can only post content that doesn't violate someone else's intellectual property rights. Infringement can occur "even if you bought or downloaded the content," and crediting the owner or not profiting doesn't change this. — search summary of [Instagram Help Center](https://help.Instagram.com/354736791367645); [copyrightlaws.com](https://copyrightlaws.com/instagram-and-copyright/)
- **Repeat infringers:** Instagram may disable accounts that repeatedly post infringing content. — search summary of [iPleaders](https://blog.ipleaders.in/everything-you-should-know-about-copyright-issues-while-posting-pictures-on-instagram/)
- **2026 originality expansion:** On 30 April 2026, Instagram extended its unoriginal-content rule to photos and carousels. Accounts that mostly share others' work "will no longer be eligible for recommendations across the app." Existing followers still see the posts. — [TechCrunch](https://techcrunch.com/2026/04/30/instagram-restricts-reach-of-content-aggregators-in-new-crackdown/); [Tubefilter](https://tubefilter.com/2026/04/30/instagram-removes-algorithm-recommendations-repost-content-aggregator)
- **Mosseri:** "If most of what you post to Instagram is someone else's content, your account is no longer going to be recommendable." Output is judged over about a month. — search summaries of [PetaPixel](https://petapixel.com/2026/04/30/new-instagram-policies-target-reposted-content/) and [Tubefilter](https://tubefilter.com/2026/04/30/instagram-removes-algorithm-recommendations-repost-content-aggregator)
- **What counts as original:** Edited third-party material can count if meaningfully transformed, for example by adding contextual on-screen text or original graphics that provide new information. Watermarks, minor crops or basic reposts are unlikely to qualify. — search summaries of [TechCrunch](https://techcrunch.com/2026/04/30/instagram-restricts-reach-of-content-aggregators-in-new-crackdown/) and [PetaPixel](https://petapixel.com/2026/04/30/new-instagram-policies-target-reposted-content/)
- **Earlier change (2024):** In 2024, Instagram began replacing recommended reposts with originals and removing aggregator posts from recommendations. — [Gigazine](https://www.gigazine.net/gsc_news/en/20240502-instagram-updated-algorithm-prioritizes-original-content); [Planoly](https://www.planoly.com/blog/instagram-updates-its-original-content-policy.md)
- **Unverified claim:** A marketing newsletter claims a "10+ reposts in 30 days" threshold. This is **unverified** and not confirmed by Instagram. — [Milled/PostPlanner](https://milled.com/postplanner/igs-new-30-day-rule-will-kill-50-of-accounts-ejL99Dh0UIX5mqVE)

### Inferences
- **Reach risk:** Redrawn carousels built on Starter Story facts, with ZeroAI's own doodle design and its own lesson or angle, are likely to count as original. Screenshots or cut-outs of Starter Story video frames and photos look much more like reposts. If they make up most of the feed, ZeroAI could lose Explore and recommendation reach, which a small brand depends on.
- **Branded content tools:** Instagram's Branded Content / "Paid partnership" label is for creators posting content they were paid for by a brand. It does not apply to ZeroAI posting its own ads. If a founder is paid to co-post or appear, the label (and FTC/ASCI-style disclosure) would apply.

## 5. Safer alternatives and a permission template

### Takeaway
The safest pattern is: **get the founder's written OK** (they usually benefit from the exposure), **use their own public post or quote with a link**, **draw them as a doodle avatar instead of using a photo**, **restate facts in ZeroAI's words**, and **keep the ZeroAI CTA visually and verbally separate**, with a "not affiliated / no endorsement implied" line. Ask Starter Story or HubSpot Media separately if ZeroAI wants to use their wording, branding or frames.

### Cited Findings
- **Where the Starter Story licence goes:** Starter Story's licence over founder contributions runs to Starter Story, so founders keep their own facts and their own posts on X, and can grant permission for those. — [Starter Story Terms](https://www.starterstory.com/terms)
- **Facts are free:** Facts, names and short phrases are not protected by copyright. — [Feist summary](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Feist_Publications,_Inc._v._Rural_Telephone_Service_Co.); [Copyright Office Circular 33](https://www.copyright.gov/circs/circ33.pdf)
- **Transformation for originality:** Instagram treats added context and original graphics as meaningful transformation. — [TechCrunch](https://techcrunch.com/2026/04/30/instagram-restricts-reach-of-content-aggregators-in-new-crackdown/)
- **Commentary vs. ads in India:** Indian courts have protected non-derogatory commentary, memes and parody under Art. 19(1)(a) (Jackie Shroff), but commercial persona use gets injunctions. — [SpicyIP](https://spicyip.com/2025/06/delhi-hcs-orders-in-sadhguru-ankur-warikoo-cases-what-indian-courts-are-getting-wrong-about-personality-rights.html)

### Inferences: a practical risk ladder (my synthesis)
| Element | Risk without permission | Safer version |
|---|---|---|
| Founder name + revenue + tech stack, in ZeroAI's own words | Low copyright risk. Some ToS/contract risk. Some persona risk if tied to the CTA | Paraphrase, add "via Starter Story" plus link, verify the number against the founder's own public posts (Starter Story says its data may be inaccurate) |
| Short verbatim quote from a Starter Story article or video | Medium | Use the founder's own public X/LinkedIn post instead, with a link, ideally with their OK |
| Founder photo or video frame cut out onto a doodle | **High** (copyright of photographer/Starter Story + personality rights) | Original doodle or illustrated avatar drawn by ZeroAI, or a photo the founder sends with written permission |
| Starter Story logo/branding on slide | Medium (trademark, implied partnership) | Plain-text credit "Data: Starter Story interview (link)", no logo |
| ZeroAI CTA in the same carousel as a founder | **High** implied-endorsement risk | Put the CTA on a separate final slide with distinct styling. Avoid copy like "they use X, you should use ZeroAI". Add "[Founder] is not affiliated with and does not endorse ZeroAI." Better still, get the founder's consent |
| Many carousels that are mostly others' material | Reach penalty under the April 2026 rule | Make each post ZeroAI's own lesson, framework or analysis, with founder facts as supporting evidence |

- **Disclaimers:** A "not affiliated" disclaimer helps against implied endorsement but **does not** cure copyright infringement or lack of consent for face use in an ad. A credit does not cure infringement either (Instagram's help centre says so explicitly).
- **Avatars:** An illustrated avatar that is clearly recognisable as the founder still uses their likeness, and California's SB 683 and the Delhi HC orders cover likeness and digital replicas. It removes the photo-copyright problem, not the persona problem. Pair it with consent, or with a generic, non-identifying character.
- **Permission is cheap:** Indie founders featured on Starter Story usually want distribution. Asking takes one DM, and a "yes" turns the post into a collaboration: they may share it, and Instagram's Collab feature can tag them.

### Template: permission DM or email to a founder
> Hi [Name], I'm [Your name] from ZeroAI (we build [one-line description] for developers in India). We loved your story about building [Product] to [$X/mo] (we found it via Starter Story).
>
> We'd like to feature you in an Instagram carousel on @zeroai about [angle, e.g. "how solo devs protect deep-work time"]. It would include:
> - your name, [Product], your revenue figure ([$X/mo], please correct it if it's changed) and your stack ([stack])
> - this quote from you: "[quote]" (or a line of your choice)
> - [a doodle-style illustration of you / a photo you're happy for us to use, which you'd send us]
>
> The last slide mentions ZeroAI, and we'll clearly state you're not affiliated with us and aren't endorsing us. We'll tag you and link your product. Happy to send a draft before posting, and we'll take it down any time you ask.
>
> Is that OK with you? A quick "yes, approved" reply is all we need. Thanks!

Keep the written reply (screenshot plus date). If ZeroAI wants to use Starter Story's wording, logo, photos or video frames, send a similar short request to Starter Story or HubSpot Media. No public permissions address was found; the DMCA contact in their policy is for infringement notices, not licensing.

### Gaps
- I found no published data on how often indie founders agree to such requests. "Founders often agree" is a plausible assumption, not a sourced statistic.
- I did not verify whether ASCI guidelines (India) require specific disclosure wording for testimonial-style social ads.
