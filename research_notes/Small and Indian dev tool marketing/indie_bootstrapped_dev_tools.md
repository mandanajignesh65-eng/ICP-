# How Indie, Bootstrapped and Very Small (Solo to ~20 People) Developer Productivity Tools Get Famous, Get Reach and Make Money

Research date: 2026-10-15. Method note: WebFetch failed with DNS errors (`getaddrinfo ENOTFOUND`) on every domain tried (tuple.app, blog.kapeli.com, starterstory.com, systemaic.com, indiehackers.com, alternativeto.net). **All findings below come from search-result snippets and summaries, not full-page reads.** Numbers that came only through a snippet should be checked against the original before anyone quotes them. Labels used: **[FR]** = founder-reported (founder's own post, tweet, blog, or interview); **[3P]** = third-party write-up repeating founder numbers; **[EST]** = aggregator estimate (Latka, RocketReach, teardown sites), which is low reliability; **[VER]** = verified by a payment-verified tracker. I found no TrustMRR-verified figure for any tool on the list.

## 1. Case studies: team size, revenue milestones, first users, main traffic sources

### Takeaway
The best-documented small paid dev tools fall into two groups. The first is **audience-first founders** (Marc Lou/ShipFast, Tony Dinh/DevUtils, Ben Orenstein/Tuple, Adam Pietrasiak/Screen Studio). They made five figures in month one because they had an X/Twitter or podcast audience before launch. The second is **quiet craft apps** (Dash, Fork, TablePlus, Proxyman, Shottr, Polypane, Wallaby). These grew slowly through word of mouth, Mac-ecosystem press and Setapp, and almost never publish revenue. Firm founder-reported numbers exist mostly for the first group.

### Inferences
- First-month results track pre-existing audience more than product. ShipFast (40K X followers, about half of traffic from his profile), Tuple (Ben's podcast audience, $8K pre-product) and DevUtils/Xnapper (10K followers built deliberately) all had distribution before launch. Screen Studio's ~$30K first month is the exception that went viral mostly on the product's own visual demo output.
- Mac-native craft tools (Dash, Fork, TablePlus, Proxyman, CleanShot, Shottr) are the long-lived bootstrapped cash flows, but they rarely disclose revenue. Their channel stack is Mac press/blogs (mjtsai, MacStories, Six Colors), Setapp and word of mouth rather than build-in-public.
- Boilerplates are the most transparent category because founders sell to indie hackers who reward revenue disclosure. They are also the most volatile: ShipFast went from a ~$133K month (Apr 2024) to a reported ~$3.5K month (date unknown, Streakr).

## 2. What content got reach

### Takeaway
What got reach was (a) the product's own output as the demo (Screen Studio, Xnapper, Ray.so/Carbon-style images that spread every time someone shares them), (b) revenue-milestone posts with exact numbers (Marc Lou), (c) Show HN / Product Hunt launches by founders with existing followings, (d) open-sourcing (Dub, Hoppscotch, Plausible, OpenStatus), and (e) "X alternative" SEO and comparison content (Plausible). Controversy also drove sales in at least one case (Dash vs Apple).

### Cited Findings
- **Product-as-demo:** Screen Studio is used to make viral feature-demo videos for X. A Starter Story Feb 2026 episode describes a founder using it to make "viral feature demonstration videos" for X growth. Screen Studio's own site leans on user tweets as social proof — [Starter Story](https://www.starterstory.com/screen-studio-breakdown); [Screen Studio](https://www.screen.studio/create/product-demo-videos)
- **Revenue-milestone posts:** Marc Lou's milestone tweet bundled revenue ($300K), visitors (329,427), customers (1,851), margin (92%) and launch date — [X @marclou](https://x.com/marclou/status/1767932387802100012)
- **Build-in-public before launch:** Marc spent ~2 years building a following before ShipFast. More than half of post-launch traffic came from his X profile — [SpyMetrics](https://spymetrics.beehiiv.com/p/ship-fast)
- **Launch-day mechanics:** Write-ups credit replying to 100+ Product Hunt comments, a funny promo video, and a $50-off code for the first 50 buyers. The landing page CTA went straight to Stripe checkout with the discount pre-applied — [SpyMetrics](https://spymetrics.beehiiv.com/p/ship-fast); [Indie Hackers](https://www.indiehackers.com/post/marc-louvion-s-unconventional-approach-to-marketing-that-grew-ship-fast-to-43k-monthly-revenue-in-2-months-6163fb113f); [gitconnected](https://levelup.gitconnected.com/how-to-get-25-000-visitors-from-hacker-news-solo-founder-marc-lous-exact-formula-17cd13ce8376)
- **Hacker News launches:** Divjoy got 5K+ exports in a single day off HN (2019). Plausible got its early spike from HN. Screen Studio's founder posted on HN around launch — [DEV](https://dev.to/gabe_ragland/divjoy-in-2019-and-what-s-coming-in-2020-3n0n); [Plausible](https://ingest.plausible.io/blog/bootstrapping-saas); [Systemaic](https://www.systemaic.com/teardowns/screen-studio)
- **Reddit:** Supastarter's first sale came 3–4 days after a Reddit post — [Starter Story](https://www.starterstory.com/supastarter-breakdown)
- **Viral tweet → sales:** an IH founder's 3D-mockup tool tweet went viral, with "sales came in every few minutes" and $7K in month one — [Indie Hackers](https://www.indiehackers.com/post/viral-tweet-and-7k-in-revenue-50a9af505f)
- **Open source as distribution:** Dub got 14K→18K stars and positioned itself as "the open-source Bitly successor". Hoppscotch went from 25K stars (Dec 2020) to 60K+. Raycast open-sourced ray.so (July 2024). Carbon is MIT and free — [OSS newsletter](https://oss.beehiiv.com/p/dub); [DEV](https://dev.to/liyasthomas/how-hoppscotch-is-building-an-open-source-api-development-ecosystem-5cl7); [AlternativeTo](https://alternativeto.net/news/2024/7/ray-so-goes-open-source-and-unifies-platforms); [AlternativeTo Ray.so](https://alternativeto.net/software/ray-so/about)
- **"Alternative" SEO / comparison pages:** Plausible targeted "Google Analytics alternative" keywords because searchers have already decided to switch. Hoppscotch is described everywhere as the "open-source Postman alternative" — [Plausible](https://ingest.plausible.io/blog/open-source-saas); [Menon Lab](https://themenonlab.blog/blog/hoppscotch-open-source-postman-alternative)
- **Viral loops in the product:** Better Stack's free status pages carried a "Powered by Better Stack" footer link. Jam's collaborative bug links spread through PLG — [Stork.ai](https://www.stork.ai/blog/de/the-footer-link-that-built-a-unicorn); [Calyx](https://calyx.substack.com/p/growing-a-devtools-startup-without)
- **Controversy:** Dash's 2016 public fight with Apple likely contributed to direct sales doubling after removal — [Dice](https://insights.dice.com/2017/02/14/developers-need-mac-app-store/)
- **Doing things that don't scale:** Mintlify hand-migrated docs and built custom docs for cold prospects — [Mintlify](https://www.mintlify.com/blog/things-that-do-not-scale)

### Inferences
- For visual tools (screen recorders, screenshot beautifiers, code-image tools), the output is the ad. Every user-shared clip watermarks or advertises the tool by its recognizable look. This explains why Screen Studio, Xnapper, CleanShot, ray.so and Carbon dominate X dev-tool reach relative to non-visual tools like Wallaby or Fork.
- Revenue screenshots get reach mainly among other indie hackers. That audience is exactly the boilerplate buyer, which is why the tactic works for ShipFast but would likely do little for a git client.

### Gaps
- No primary data on YouTube reviews by small creators driving sales for any of these tools.
- No first-party traffic-source breakdowns (other than ShipFast's "half from X") were found.

## 3. Pricing tactics that made developers pay

### Takeaway
The dominant pattern for small dev tools is a **low one-time license** ($8–$229) with paid update renewals, plus optional Setapp distribution. Subscriptions get adopted later and often provoke backlash (Tower 2018, Screen Studio Oct 2025). Boilerplates sell at $129–$299 one-time with launch discount codes. Free-to-paid conversions (Shottr) and freemium funnels (Quokka → Wallaby; Proxyman's limited free tier) are common. AppSumo lifetime deals carry reputational risk.

### Cited Findings
- **Screen Studio went subscription-only (Oct 2025):** removed the $229 one-time license (1 year of updates) for new buyers. Current pricing: $29/mo or $108/yr. Existing license holders get updates through Sept 25, 2027. The founder said $229 "was not justified", called the tier a mistake with low adoption, and said bootstrapping requires recurring revenue. Users called it misleading on Reddit — [AlternativeTo](https://alternativeto.net/news/2025/10/screen-studio-has-removed-their-non-subscription-licensing-options-for-new-customers/); [Trainn](https://trainn.co/blog/screen-studio-pricing/); [VibrantSnap](https://www.vibrantsnap.com/blog/screen-studio-alternatives)
- **Screen Studio promo:** a 50%-off promo day in Dec 2022 was its biggest sales day, beating Product Hunt launch day — [X @pie6k](https://x.com/pie6k/status/1610782639841226752)
- **Tower switched to subscription (2018):** $79 one-time → $69/yr. Justification: ship bigger features faster. Free for students and educators — [mjtsai](https://mjtsai.com/blog/2018/06/26/git-tower-3-switches-to-subscription); [Tower](https://www.git-tower.com/docs/ai/product-overview/)
- **One-time licenses still common:** Fork $59.99, TablePlus ~$89, Proxyman ~$49, CleanShot ~$29, Xnapper $29/device, Shottr $8/$25. Tony Dinh says most of his $45K/mo comes from one-time purchases — [Gappsy](https://gappsy.com/tools/fork/); [Enterno](https://enterno.io/s/alternatives-tableplus); [ifun.de](https://www.ifun.de/proxyman-native-mac-app-zeigt-https-verbindungen-139672/); [TechRadar](https://www.techradar.com/reviews/cleanshot-x-for-mac-review); [Indie Hackers](https://www.indiehackers.com/post/8k-revenue-from-building-a-screenshot-app-in-2022-41aa4cc754); [ScreenSnap](https://www.screensnap.pro/blog/shottr-mac-review); [Indie Hackers](https://www.indiehackers.com/post/my-solopreneur-story-zero-to-45k-mo-in-2-years-1b858f47af)
- **Boilerplate pricing:** ShipFast one-time ~$129–$149 at launch with lifetime updates, and a 92% margin. Supastarter is one-time — [SpyMetrics](https://spymetrics.beehiiv.com/p/ship-fast); [X @marclou](https://x.com/marclou/status/1767932387802100012); [Starter Story](https://www.starterstory.com/supastarter-breakdown)
- **Setapp economics:** launched 2017 at $9.99/mo. Developers get ~70% of revenue split by usage. A 2016–17 plan reserved part of MacPaw's 30% to reward developers who bring users, and 2023 reporting described up to 90% for developer-referred customers. CleanShot X and Proxyman are both in Setapp. Current terms are not confirmed — [MacTrast](https://www.mactrast.com/2017/01/can-eat-mac-app-subscription-service-setapp-goes-live-60-apps-9-99-per-month/); [The Next Web](https://thenextweb.com/news/setapp-wants-netflix-macos-applications); [AlternativeTo](https://alternativeto.net/news/2023/8/setapp-plans-independent-ios-app-store-for-eu-users-awaiting-digital-markets-act-approval/); [Brett Terpstra](https://brettterpstra.com/2016/11/09/setapp-sustainable-recurring-revenue-for-mac-developers/)
- **Direct sales vs App Store:** Dash's direct sales doubled after the App Store removal. Rogue Amoeba's Piezo test also earned more outside the Mac App Store. 70% of Mac developers selling outside the store cited its revenue model (MacPaw 2019 survey) — [Rogue Amoeba](https://weblog.rogueamoeba.com/2017/02/10/piezos-life-outside-the-app-store/); [Setapp survey](https://setapp.com/news/mac-developers-survey-2019)
- **AppSumo / lifetime deals:** Sizzy's AppSumo buyers later complained of abandonment ("another wrong purchase"). An IH founder's AppSumo post is titled "The $45K sugar rush: what $110K in AppSumo gross revenue taught us", and AppSumo keeps a large cut. Another IH founder paused an LTD to grow MRR. Rob Walling's essay warns that LTDs hurt SaaS businesses — [AppSumo](https://appsumo.com/products/sizzy/reviews/another-wrong-purchase-on-appsumo-324846/); [Indie Hackers](https://indiehackers.com/post/the-45k-sugar-rush-what-110k-in-appsumo-gross-revenue-taught-us-0383952b35); [Indie Hackers](https://www.indiehackers.com/product/glorify-2/pausing-our-lifetime-deal-to-take-stab-at-growing-mrr--NDSnhfQ4C3L5nbNb0_m); [Bootstrapped Founder](https://thebootstrappedfounder.com/lifetime-deals-and-saas-businesses/)
- **PPP discounts:** vendor claims only. ParityDeals claims ~15% revenue increase, and a guide cites 20–70% more sales from low-PPP regions. One indie dev reported low-PPP revenue share up 800% with no baseline. No independent study found — [ParityDeals](https://www.paritydeals.com/features/purchasing-power-parity-discounts); [Fungies](https://fungies.io/purchasing-power-parity-saas-pricing-2026/); [Dodo Payments](https://dodopayments.com/distribution/purchasing-power-parity)
- **Team/enterprise ("expense it"):** Tuple's enterprise sales became "an important part of their business model". Greptile and Kilo sell per-seat team plans ($15/user/mo for Kilo) — [Hackers Incorporated](https://metacast.app/podcast/hackers-incorporated/be9kNw2q); [Rywalker](https://rywalker.com/research/kilo)
- **Anti-piracy stance:** Screen Studio deliberately does not fight cracks — [X @pie6k](https://x.com/pie6k/status/1847026857935266283?lang=en)
- **Free → paid transition:** Shottr went from free/donation to $8 one-time. Divjoy started charging for code export after a free HN viral moment — [ScreenSnap](https://www.screensnap.pro/blog/shottr-mac-review); [DEV](https://dev.to/gabe_ragland/divjoy-in-2019-and-what-s-coming-in-2020-3n0n)

### Inferences
- Individual developers pay for a one-time, low-friction license tied to an immediate visible time saving. Recurring revenue mostly comes from teams/companies (Tuple, Tower per-user, Greptile, Kilo Teams) where the buyer expenses it.
- The one-time-to-subscription migration is the most common pricing crisis point for small tools. Both Tower (2018) and Screen Studio (2025) moved for "sustainable development" reasons, and both took public criticism.

### Gaps
- No founder-reported conversion data on student discounts or PPP for any named tool.
- No data on what share of CleanShot/Proxyman revenue comes from Setapp vs direct.

## 4. Affiliate and referral programs

### Takeaway
Evidence is thin. ShipFast runs a 30–50% tiered affiliate program, but no founder-reported affiliate revenue share was found. For most small dev tools, the "referral" engine is in-product virality (shared videos, footer links, collaborative links) and audience, not paid affiliates.

### Cited Findings
- ShipFast affiliate program: 30–50% commission, tiered, 30-day cookie (directory listing updated May 31, 2026) — [RevShare](https://www.revshare.so/programs/advertiser/shipfast)
- Setapp's model rewards developers with an extra share for customers they bring onto the platform (a referral bonus inside a bundle) — [The Next Web](https://thenextweb.com/news/setapp-wants-netflix-macos-applications); [AlternativeTo](https://alternativeto.net/news/2023/8/setapp-plans-independent-ios-app-store-for-eu-users-awaiting-digital-markets-act-approval/)
- In-product referral loops: Better Stack's "Powered by" footer and Jam's PLG collaborative sharing — [Stork.ai](https://www.stork.ai/blog/de/the-footer-link-that-built-a-unicorn); [Calyx](https://calyx.substack.com/p/growing-a-devtools-startup-without)
- Tweet Hunter (a tool for X creators, not a dev tool): "every time we made sales, it was coming from my Twitter followers", meaning the founder's audience, not affiliates — [Indie Hackers](https://www.indiehackers.com/post/i-built-tweet-hunters-mvp-in-2-weeks-4-months-later-it-grew-to-150k-arr-ama-a6ae1ee850)

### Inferences
- Affiliate programs fit products whose buyers are themselves creators with audiences (boilerplates sold to indie hackers who tweet). Mac utilities and git clients rely on Setapp and organic reviews instead.

### Gaps
- No founder-reported data on affiliate share of revenue for ShipFast, Screen Studio, Supastarter or any other tool. Screen Studio's affiliate terms were not found.

## 5. Failures and cautionary cases: attention without revenue

### Takeaway
The recurring failure pattern is a viral launch or a big star count on a **free** product with weak retention or no paid wedge. Examples: Figstack's 5,000 day-one signups that churned, Fig's large free user base sold to Amazon, OpenStatus's "small MRR" not covering costs, and Roo Code shutting down. Lifetime deals create a second failure mode: cash up front, resentful users later (Sizzy).

### Cited Findings
- **Fig (YC, ~$2M+ raised):** a free terminal autocomplete with wide developer love. Acquired by Amazon in 2023. New signups closed, then all Fig products were sunset and folded into CodeWhisperer CLI / Amazon Q. The search found no monetization post-mortem — [TechCrunch](https://techcrunch.com/?p=2591683); [GitHub discussion](https://fr.github.com/withfig/autocomplete/issues/2068)
- **Figstack (pre-Mintlify):** viral Product Hunt launch with 5,000 day-1 signups, then usage dropped. Two months of fixes failed. Lesson: "5,000 signups mean nothing without retention" — [Focal podcast](https://focalpodcast.transistor.fm/episodes/why-betting-on-an-idea-space-beats-a-single-idea-why-5-000-signups-mean-nothing-without-retention-how-to-know-when-to-pivot-vs-persist-how-to-survive-14-months-of-failure-why-instincts-beat-market-research-han-wang-co-founder-ceo-of-mintlify); [Pascal's Notes](https://pascalsnotes.substack.com/p/if-when-and-how-to-pivot-lessons)
- **OpenStatus:** open source with many GitHub fans, but as of the 2023 year-end it was "still not profitable" and its MRR did not cover costs — [OpenStatus](https://www.openstatus.dev/blog/2023-year-review)
- **Divjoy:** 5K exports in one day from HN. The founder had to add paid export to make it sustainable, and no evidence of large revenue followed. LinkedIn lists 1 employee — [DEV](https://dev.to/gabe_ragland/divjoy-in-2019-and-what-s-coming-in-2020-3n0n); [LinkedIn](https://www.linkedin.com/company/divjoy)
- **Roo Code:** a popular open-source AI coding extension. [3P] Shut down May 15, 2026 to pivot. Kilo forked it and raised money with a credits/teams model — [Rywalker](https://rywalker.com/research/kilo)
- **ShipFast decay:** [3P] from a ~$133K month (Apr 2024) to a reported $3,537 month (date not visible). Marc's total 2025 income was 20% below 2024. Hype-driven one-time products decay without a recurring base — [The Builder OS](https://thebuilderos.beehiiv.com/p/marc-lou-crosses-133kmo-simple-nextjs-boilerplate); [Streakr](https://streakr.co/playbook/marc-lou)
- **Sizzy LTD backlash:** AppSumo buyers felt abandoned. LTD revenue does not fund ongoing maintenance — [AppSumo](https://appsumo.com/products/sizzy/reviews/another-wrong-purchase-on-appsumo-324846/)
- **Attention ≠ income:** the IH "MRR progress bar in profile" micro-trend went viral but was "just a tool to show off" — [Indie Hackers](https://www.indiehackers.com/post/mrr-progress-bar-in-profile-is-it-becoming-viral-47dcb1edad)
- **Contrast, VC-scale small teams:** Greptile (~20 people) reports "eight-figure ARR" with a two-person GTM team, so small headcount does not mean indie economics when there is VC money and an AI-era enterprise budget — [YC](https://ycombinator.com/companies/greptile); [startup.jobs](https://startup.jobs/revops-greptile-9617171)

### Inferences
- Likes and stars convert when (1) there is a paid wedge on day one (ShipFast checkout link, Screen Studio paid app, Tuple charging pre-product) and (2) the buyer has a budget or the price is below the impulse threshold. Free-first tools with no clear upgrade path (Fig, Figstack, OpenStatus early) accumulate attention but not revenue.
- Audience-driven one-time-sale products are exposed to decay. Founders hedge by stacking products (Marc Lou ~15 income streams, Tony Dinh 3 products) or by switching to subscription (Screen Studio).

### Gaps
- No founder post-mortem from Fig explaining monetization was found in search.
- Search found no reports of layoffs or failure for Swimm or Pieces. Their status is unverified.
