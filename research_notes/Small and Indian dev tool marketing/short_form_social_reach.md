# Short-form social reach for small and Indian dev tools (Instagram, TikTok, YouTube Shorts, LinkedIn) and developer-focused Instagram pages

Method note (October 2026): about 30 web searches. Fetching pages directly failed: instagram.com and collabstr.com did not resolve from the sandbox ("getaddrinfo ENOTFOUND"). So every number below comes from search-result snippets, aggregator profiles (Qoruz, Socialveins, Heepsy, Scrumball, CreatorDB, Collabstr), or self-reported founder and employee posts. All follower and view counts are approximate. Dates are given where the source showed one. Many snapshots are undated, so check live profiles before reusing any figure.

## Q1. Which small or mid-size dev tools and AI dev tools post on Instagram, TikTok or Shorts and get traction?

### Takeaway
Most small dev tools have tiny brand accounts on Instagram and TikTok, roughly hundreds to a few thousand followers. The ones that get big short-form reach do it by paying many outside creators per view (Composio), or by running a dedicated skit or comedy account and posting hundreds of videos (tl;dv, a small B2B AI tool, used here as the closest well-documented example). A small brand posting its own demos rarely gets there. Founder personal accounts such as Dhravya Shah's @dhravya.dev exist, but I found no public numbers for them.

### Cited Findings
**Brand accounts of small dev tools (small numbers)**
- Jam.dev on TikTok (@jamdotdev): about 1,666 followers and 23.5K total likes (undated snapshot). Posts include a Config 2025 clip. Tagline: "1 click to capture a perfect bug report." — [TikTok @jamdotdev](https://www.tiktok.com/@jamdotdev)
- Screen Studio on Instagram (@screenstudioapp): about 693 followers and 15 posts in a cached, undated snapshot. The product itself sells "Reel mode" for making Instagram and TikTok tutorial videos. — [Instagram @screenstudioapp](https://www.instagram.com/screenstudioapp/); [Screen Studio, Instagram tutorials](https://screen.studio/create/instagram-tutorials)
- Keploy, Hoppscotch, Requestly, Middleware and CodeAnt: searches found no Instagram or TikTok traction data for any of them. Their visible social activity is on LinkedIn, GitHub and campus programs (see Q3 and Q5). — searches returned only generic Reels guides, for example [Metricool Reels guide](https://metricool.com/instagram-reels-guide/)

**Composio (Indian-founded AI agent integration platform, now SF-based Series A): creator program is the standout case**
- A Composio employee, Victor Guo, says on LinkedIn that the creator program passed about 200M views in about 3 months. He estimates that reached about 50M unique people. An earlier post said 100M views in 2 months. He describes the program as going "from 0 to 100 creators and 150M views", and says his own first video for Composio hit 4M views. — [Victor Guo, LinkedIn](https://www.linkedin.com/in/victorg8182/)
- His reported program stats: 100+ active creators, a $1 CPM, 4,000+ videos posted, a 4% engagement rate, and a mainly US audience with an average age of about 24. — [Victor Guo, LinkedIn](https://www.linkedin.com/in/victorg8182/)
- On signups, he says organic social is "one of our biggest growth drivers" and "brings in signups cheaper than any other channel we run". He also says it drives "thousands of new users daily". All of this is self-reported, with no independent attribution data. — [Victor Guo, LinkedIn](https://www.linkedin.com/in/victorg8182/)
- Composio also runs a public affiliate program that pays $5 per activated user. — [Composio affiliate program (Dub Partners)](https://partners.dub.co/composio-dev)
- CEO Soham Ganatra reposted a claim that SDK downloads grew 50x this year. — [Soham Ganatra, LinkedIn](https://www.linkedin.com/in/soham-ganatra/)

**tl;dv (small B2B AI meeting-recorder, a close analogue for a dev tool)**
- Its English TikTok account has about 797 videos, about 68M total views, and a median of about 25K views per video. A Spanish account that reposts AI-dubbed videos added about 10M views. These figures come from a third-party analysis. — [Chromatic Labs](https://www.chromaticlabs.co/blog/how-tl-dv-boosted-tiktok-views-by-10-million-using-ai-dubbing)
- The early goal was brand awareness, with signups expected later. A scripted sketch about one-on-one meetings got about 300K views. After about 6 months the account was gaining 10K+ followers per month. The signup form asks "how did you hear about us", and the team sees signup spikes that line up with view spikes. Their rule of thumb is to remake whatever works, and they treat a like rate above about 10% as a strong signal. — [The TikTok Sauce interview](https://thetiktoksauce.substack.com/p/how-tldv-cracked-b2b-tiktok-with)
- Lenny's Newsletter reports that tl;dv got up to about a third of signups from social, driven by its TikTok sketch-comedy series. It also pays creators $100 per 20K views, capped at $700 per post. — [Lenny's Newsletter, 60 creative growth ideas](https://www.lennysnewsletter.com/p/60-creative-growth-ideas)

**Contrast with larger B2B brands (brief)**
- ClickUp runs a workplace sketch-comedy account, and one video reached about 19M views. Semrush's recurring "Chris" character has passed 10M views. — [Sculpt, B2B TikTok examples](https://wearesculpt.com/blog/b2b-brand-on-tik-tok/)
- Lovable ran a Whop "Content Rewards" campaign paying $2 per 1K views on a $10K budget. — [Whop blog](https://whop.com/blog/content-rewards-x-lovable/)

**Founder-creators**
- Dhravya Shah (Supermemory, Indian-born, 19 to 20 years old) has an Instagram account, "Dhravya Shah (the DEV guy)" (@dhravya.dev). I could not find a follower count or reel view counts. — [Instagram @dhravya.dev](https://www.instagram.com/dhravya.dev/)
- Supermemory raised about $3M in seed funding from backers including Jeff Dean and Logan Kilpatrick. — [ASU / TechCrunch coverage](https://entrepreneurship.engineering.asu.edu/2025/10/asus-dhravya-shah-featured-in-techcrunch-19-year-old-raises-google-backed-funding-for-ai-startup-supermemory/)
- Supermemory open-sourced its engine, and the GitHub repo has passed 26K stars. — [Solo Founders profile](https://solofounders.com/blog/he-sold-a-company-at-16-raised-3m-at-19-then-gave-his-ai-engine-away-for-free/)
- Dhravya's X account describes an outbound play that went beyond short video: a lead list of about 123K startup founders and engineers, AI handling outbound replies, and a reported 4.2% reply rate (self-reported). — [Dhravya Shah on X](https://x.com/DhravyaShah?lang=en)

**Indie or solo examples on TikTok (thin evidence)**
- An Indie Hackers thread cites "17k signups in one year just uploading TikTok videos", with no product named. Another commenter says many SaaS apps "really fail" on TikTok. — [Indie Hackers thread](https://www.indiehackers.com/post/is-anyone-successfully-using-tiktok-to-grow-their-saas-business-a08aafeebb)
- prompt-genie.com: one video went "mega viral" and the account passed 2K followers, but the founder reported only about 300 users and $0 MRR at the time of writing. — [Indie Hackers](https://www.indiehackers.com/post/embracing-tiktok-as-an-indie-hacker-9108458439)

### Inferences
- For a small dev tool, buying distribution through outside creators paid per view ($1 to $2 CPM) is the proven way to get large view counts. Building a brand account from zero to large is slow: tl;dv needed about 6 months of steady skits before follower growth took off, and has posted about 800 videos.
- Composio's audience was a young, mostly US audience. That suggests mass short-form works best for tools with a broad "AI agent / vibe-coding" appeal, not niche infrastructure tools.
- Founder-led Instagram, as with Dhravya, looks more like personal brand-building than measured acquisition. The founders who publish numbers (Composio, Supermemory) attribute their growth to creator programs, open source and outbound, not to the founder's own reels.

### Gaps
- No follower or view numbers found for @dhravya.dev, or for any Instagram or TikTok presence of Keploy, Middleware, CodeAnt, Hoppscotch or Requestly.
- No independently verified view-to-signup conversion rate for any dev tool. Composio's and tl;dv's figures are self-reported.
- No TikTok account size found for Screen Studio. The Instagram snapshot is undated.

Global coding-meme pages range from about 12K to 124K followers. Indian meme-page shoutouts are cheap, from a few hundred to a few thousand rupees. I found no hard evidence of any dev tool getting a specific number of signups from one of these pages.

**How these pages grow and monetize**
- Comment-to-DM ("comment TOOLS and I'll DM you the link") is the standard tactic, because Instagram suppresses links. A HypeAuditor and ManyChat study says creators using DM automation grew followers faster in every tier; one vendor summary says up to 140% faster year over year. This is vendor-influenced data. — [HypeAuditor x ManyChat](https://hypeauditor.com/manychat/); [Eden](https://eden.so/blog/instagram-comment-to-dm/)
- Indian meme-page shoutout rates (from a seller's blog, 2026): 10K to 50K pages charge ₹150 to ₹400 per story and ₹400 to ₹1,000 per post. 50K to 100K pages charge ₹300 to ₹800 per story and ₹800 to ₹2,000 per post. 500K+ pages charge ₹3,000 to ₹10,000+. The same blog warns of WhatsApp and Telegram scams that demand full advance payment, and says to ask for reel-view screenshots. — [instaboostpanel](https://instaboostpanel.com/blog/instagram-shoutout-price-india/)
- Indian creator reel rates (Qolab live data, 10 Oct 2026): median about ₹1,150 per reel for 1K to 10K followers and about ₹4,736 for 10K to 50K. — [Qolab](https://qolab.in/blog/paid-promotion-instagram-price-india)
- Other Indian benchmarks for micro creators (10K to 100K): roughly ₹6K to ₹35K per reel in one source, and up to ₹80K in another. — [Superprofile rate-card guide](https://superprofile.bio/guides/influencer-rate-card-india.md); [Zutsu Media](https://www.zutsumedia.com/post/how-much-influencers-charge-sponsored-posts-india)
- Global calculators say tech, finance and business niches command 50 to 100% higher rates. — [SocialRails calculator](https://socialrails.com/free-tools/instagram-sponsored-post-rate-calculator)

### Inferences
- An Indian coding-reel creator with 30K to 300K followers costs about $180 to $400 (about ₹15K to ₹35K) per Reel on Collabstr. That is roughly 10 to 50x what a general meme-page shoutout costs, which you'd expect given the targeted developer and student audience.
- Many Indian "tech" pages with very large followings are gadget or general-tech pages. For dev tools, the more relevant layer is coding-specific creators and edtech brands (CodeWithHarry, Sheryians), whose audience skews toward students.

### Gaps
- No verified handles or counts found for global "AI tools you didn't know existed" faceless pages. The search results were polluted by AI-influencer and vendor lists.
- No public rate card found for a specific AI-tools page. No case study of a dev tool's signups from a single Instagram page promotion.
- Page networks (one owner running many pages) and collab posts as growth tactics were not documented in the sources I found.

## Q3. LinkedIn: small dev tool founders (global and Indian) who built reach, high-engagement formats, and numbers

### Takeaway
Indian dev tool founders use LinkedIn mainly for milestone and credibility posts: GitHub star counts, funding, and usage growth. The engagement data I could find is modest, often dozens of comments. In format studies, carousels (PDF document posts) lead on engagement rate, while text posts get the most reach.

### Cited Findings
- Liyas Thomas (Hoppscotch) posts GitHub-star milestones regularly: 33,333 stars (12 comments), 52,000 stars, and a later post at about 70,000 stars (11 comments). A profile snippet mentions about 79K stars. He cross-posts milestones to DEV.to and Medium at 25K, 30K and 35K stars. — [LinkedIn 33,333 post](https://www.linkedin.com/posts/liyasthomas_33333-github-stars-for-hoppscotch-many-activity-6868195770294779904-e2sk); [LinkedIn 52K post](https://www.linkedin.com/posts/liyasthomas_hoppscotch-just-passed-52000-stars-on-github-activity-7077690930786414592-9vHN); [DEV.to 25K](https://dev.to/liyasthomas/hoppscotch-celebrating-25-000-github-stars-5aao)
- Amartya Jha (CodeAnt AI, YC W24) has about 42K LinkedIn followers (snapshot about 22 days before Oct 2026). The CodeAnt company page has only about 1K to 2.2K followers across snapshots. Cached individual posts show modest engagement, from about 31 to about 104 reactions. — [Amartya Jha LinkedIn](https://www.linkedin.com/in/amartya-jha/); [CodeAnt LinkedIn](https://www.linkedin.com/company/codeant-ai)
- Composio uses LinkedIn for usage-growth claims (CEO's "SDK downloads 50x") and for employees narrating creator-program results (200M views). — [Soham Ganatra](https://www.linkedin.com/in/soham-ganatra/); [Victor Guo](https://www.linkedin.com/in/victorg8182/)
- Format data: Buffer (45M+ posts, 2026) puts the median LinkedIn carousel engagement rate at 21.77%, versus 7.35% for video. Carousels beat video by 196% and text by 585%. — [Buffer](https://buffer.com/resources/data-best-content-format-social-media/)
- Ordinal (about 216K posts) finds the same ranking at much lower absolute rates: carousel 2.44%, video 2.26%, image 2.11%, text 1.53%. But text posts get the most reach. — [Ordinal](https://www.tryordinal.com/blog/carousel-posts)
- LinkedIn's own claim differs: video drives 1.4x more engagement than other formats. — [Social Media Today / Socialinsider](https://www.socialmediatoday.com/news/linkedin-company-pages-best-practices-2025-socialinsider/745350/)

### Inferences
- For Indian dev tool founders, the founder's personal profile carries the reach, not the company page. CodeAnt has about 42K followers on the founder's profile against about 2K on the company page.
- "We hit X stars" posts are a reliable recurring format but get modest engagement. Their value is credibility with investors and hiring, not mass reach.

### Gaps
- No LinkedIn data found for the Requestly, Middleware or Keploy founders, or for Supermemory on LinkedIn.
- No LinkedIn post-to-signup attribution found for any small dev tool.

## Q4. Which short-form video formats work for dev tools, and any views-to-signups data?

### Takeaway
Two formats have documented results: sketch or POV comedy built around recurring characters (tl;dv, ClickUp, Semrush), and many-creator UGC or tool-showcase videos (Composio). Screen-recording demos and Shorts work as discovery and trust-building. Only anecdotal conversion data exists for them.

### Cited Findings
- Skits: tl;dv's recurring employee characters got about 68M views over about 800 videos, with a median of about 25K. Social drove up to about a third of signups. — [Chromatic Labs](https://www.chromaticlabs.co/blog/how-tl-dv-boosted-tiktok-views-by-10-million-using-ai-dubbing); [Lenny's](https://www.lennysnewsletter.com/p/60-creative-growth-ideas)
- tl;dv's team targeted product managers and sales reps, with minimal approvals for skit content. — [Sculpt](https://wearesculpt.com/blog/b2b-brand-on-tik-tok/); [The TikTok Sauce](https://thetiktoksauce.substack.com/p/how-tldv-cracked-b2b-tiktok-with)
- AI dubbing to reuse content in another language (Spanish) added about 10M views. — [Chromatic Labs](https://www.chromaticlabs.co/blog/how-tl-dv-boosted-tiktok-views-by-10-million-using-ai-dubbing)
- YouTube Shorts anecdote: a founder on Reddit (2025) said one 20-second Short got about 2,500 views and brought their first paying customer. This is secondhand and unverified. — [Fluxnote](https://fluxnote.io/guides/youtube-shorts-for-saas-startups)
- One B2B guide calls Shorts "a discovery and trust-seeding tool, not a direct closer". It cites a figure that 53% of B2B buyers watch short-form video before filling in a form, and recommends tracking pinned-comment link CTR plus UTM-attributed trials. — [Komet Media](https://www.kometmedia.com/blogs/youtube-shorts-algorithm-and-reach-explained); [Fluxnote](https://fluxnote.io/guides/youtube-shorts-for-saas-startups)
- A devtools-focused source notes that low view counts are fine when the audience is narrow and targeted. — [Stateshift](https://blog.stateshift.com/youtube-for-devtools/)
- Reels context: 61% of marketers use Reels, up from 37% in 2024 (HubSpot). Reels lead on shares. Videos over 3 minutes are reportedly not eligible for recommendations (vendor claim). — [HubSpot](https://blog.hubspot.com/marketing/instagram-marketing-report); [Metricool](https://metricool.com/instagram-reels-guide/); [TrueFuture Media](https://www.truefuturemedia.com/articles/instagram-reels-reach-2026-business-growth-guide)
- Indian coding creators sell "turning boring problems into real-life solutions" reels and visual concept explainers. These are the faceless or semi-faceless tool-list and explainer formats a dev tool would sponsor. — [Collabstr @developer_rahul_](https://collabstr.com/developerrahul); [Collabstr @this.girl.tech](https://collabstr.com/thisgirltechh)

### Inferences
- High volume plus remaking winners (tl;dv's rule: remake what works and watch for a like rate above about 10%) matters more than polish.
- For developer tools, the realistic conversion model is that views drive brand recall, which then shows up in "how did you hear about us" answers. Expect attribution through self-report surveys, not click-through.

### Gaps
- No dev-tool-specific comparison of formats (screen-recording demo vs before/after vs founder talking head) with view and signup numbers.
- No verified views-to-signup ratio for any dev tool.

## Q5. UGC and micro-influencer programs used by small dev tools, and their costs

### Takeaway
The going rate for pay-per-view creator programs is about $1 to $2 per 1,000 views (Composio $1 CPM, Lovable $2 CPM, tl;dv $100 per 20K views capped at $700). Flat UGC videos cost about $75 to $500. Indian dev-tool student programs, such as Keploy's API Fellowship, are education and community programs, not paid content programs.

### Cited Findings
- Composio: $1 CPM across 100+ creators and 4,000+ videos, reaching about 200M views in about 3 months. That implies about $200K in view payouts if every view was paid at $1 CPM. This is my arithmetic, and the actual spend was not disclosed. — [Victor Guo, LinkedIn](https://www.linkedin.com/in/victorg8182/)
- A GTM Sauce guide describes comparable app creator deals with a $1 to $2 CPM view bonus. — [GTM Sauce](https://gtmsauce.com/topics/ugc-creators)
- tl;dv pays $100 per 20K views, capped at $700 per post, which works out to $5 CPM. — [Lenny's Newsletter](https://www.lennysnewsletter.com/p/60-creative-growth-ideas)
- Lovable: Whop Content Rewards at $2 per 1K views on a $10K budget. — [Whop](https://whop.com/blog/content-rewards-x-lovable/)
- Whop Content Rewards averages about $1.25 per 1K views. The platform fee is 10% (8% for verified brands). Sources conflict on a $1K minimum budget versus no minimum. — [Shopiator](https://shopiator.com/blog/whop-content-reward-campaigns); [NewWave](https://www.new-wave.ai/blog/pay-ugc-creators-per-view); [Seenfold](https://seenfold.com/blog/best-clipping-agencies)
- Open clipping marketplaces publish CPMs but don't guarantee burst capacity or report signups against views. — [FindClout](https://findclout.com/blog/best-ai-startup-clipping)
- Flat UGC pricing: about $50 to $500 per video, with a common benchmark of about $198. Apps average about $76 per 30-second video on Influee. Usage rights add 25 to 50% per platform. — [NovoAds](https://novoads.ai/blog/how-much-do-ugc-creators-charge); [Influee](https://influee.co/industries/apps); [The Social Outline](https://thesocialoutline.com/blog/ugc-agency-pricing-mobile-apps)
- Affiliate model: Composio pays $5 per activated user. Generic AI-tool affiliates pay about $5 to $50 per conversion (unsourced blog figure). — [Composio affiliates](https://partners.dub.co/composio-dev); [Medium](https://medium.com/activated-thinker/how-to-start-a-faceless-tiktok-account-that-actually-makes-money-in-2026-81283a8b0e31)
- Keploy API Fellowship: about 2 weeks and 6 sessions on APIs, Git and GSoC preparation. It helps fellows start campus communities, with no stated pay. Keploy DevRel staff also speak at GDG on Campus events. — [Keploy Fellowship](https://fellowship.keploy.io/); [Keploy blog](https://keploy.io/blog/community/my-journey-of-keploy-fellowship-program); [GDG IIIT Kalyani](https://gdg.community.dev/events/details/google-gdg-on-campus-indian-institute-of-information-technology-kalyani-india-presents-deep-dive-into-apis-with-keploy/)

### Inferences
- A small Indian dev tool could test both models side by side on a small budget. One option is 5 to 10 Indian coding creators at $180 to $400 per Reel (Collabstr), or about ₹5K to ₹35K each at Indian rates. The other is a pay-per-view pool at $1 to $2 CPM for global reach.
- Student fellowships produce blog posts and LinkedIn posts from participants, such as the many "my Keploy fellowship journey" Medium posts. That is a cheap source of user-written content, but it isn't short-form video.

### Gaps
- No published cost-per-signup for any dev tool creator program.
- No India-specific paid ambassador or per-video program found for Keploy, Hoppscotch, Requestly, Middleware, CodeAnt or Supermemory.
