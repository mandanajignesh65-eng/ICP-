# Developer Buying Psychology and Frameworks for Selling Productivity Tools to Skeptical, Price-Sensitive, OSS-First Developers

Notes compiled 2026-10-15. Labels used below: **[DATA]** means survey, measured or official-policy data. **[OPINION]** means practitioner or analyst commentary. **[EVENT]** means a dated, documented company action. Note on method: several primary domains (survey.stackoverflow.co, stackoverflow.blog, blog.jetbrains.com, heavybit.com) could not be reached directly from this environment. Figures from those sites come from search-engine extracts of the primary pages and are flagged where relevant. Where possible, verify exact percentages against the primary page before publishing.

## 1. Survey data: discovery, purchase influence, willingness to pay, trust (2024–2026)

### Takeaway
About half of developers influence technology purchases at work: 48% in the SO 2025 survey, and older or broader SO figures say 62–66%. Developers endorse tools mainly for quality and a good API or ease of use. They reject tools first for security or privacy concerns, second for prohibitive pricing and third because a better alternative exists. Cost matters much more for personal projects than for work ones. Developers learn through documentation, not ads: 84% use technical docs. Trust in AI tools is falling even as their use rises, so AI claims carry a credibility penalty.

### Cited Findings
**Stack Overflow Developer Survey 2025 (fielded mid-2025, published July/Aug 2025)**
- [DATA] 48% of developers either endorsed the use of, or influenced the purchase of, new technology at their organization in the past year. Roughly one in five of those influenced a *major* addition to the company's tech stack. — [SO 2025 Survey: Work](https://survey.stackoverflow.co/2025/work) (via search extract)
- [DATA] Reasons to endorse a technology: "reputation for quality" ranks first overall, followed by "easy-to-use API". For **work** projects, APIs rank 1 and quality ranks 2. For **personal** projects, "costs are manageable" moves up to rank 4. On "costs are manageable" overall: rank 5, median rank 4, and mode rank 1, meaning the most common single answer put cost first. "AI integration" ranks 9th (second to last) for both work and personal projects. — [SO 2025 Survey: Work](https://survey.stackoverflow.co/2025/work)
- [DATA] Top deal-breakers for rejecting a technology, all developers: (1) security or privacy concerns, (2) prohibitive pricing, (3) better alternatives available. AI integration is the *least* important factor in rejection. — [SO 2025 Survey: Work](https://survey.stackoverflow.co/2025/work)
- [DATA, conflicting measure] Stack Overflow's advertising material cites 62% of respondents with "influence over technology purchases", and its eBook cites 66% of professional developers from the **2023** survey. These use a broader or older question than the 2025 figure of 48%. — [SO Advertising: developers gaining influence](https://stackoverflow.co/advertising/resources/discover-how-developers-are-gaining-influence-on-technology-purchases/); [SO: A seat at the table](https://stackoverflow.co/advertising/resources/seat-at-the-table)
- [DATA] AI trust, 2025: 84% use or plan to use AI tools (76% in 2024). 46% actively *distrust* AI accuracy versus 33% who trust it, and only 3% "highly trust" it. Distrust was 31% in the prior year. 66% cite "AI solutions that are almost right, but not quite" as a frustration, and 45% say debugging AI-generated code takes significant time. — [SO 2025 press release](https://stackoverflow.co/company/press/archive/stack-overflow-2025-developer-survey/); [SO 2025 AI section](https://survey.stackoverflow.co/2025/ai)
- [DATA, conflicting] SO's leaders' summary and its December 2025 post put "trust AI outputs to be accurate" at **29%**, down from 40% in 2024, while other coverage reports 33%. The two figures likely come from different question framings. — [SO for leaders: AI adoption](https://stackoverflow.co/internal/resources/2025-stack-overflow-developer-survey-for-leaders/ai-adoption/); [SO blog Dec 2025](https://stackoverflow.blog/2025/12/29/developers-remain-willing-but-reluctant-to-use-ai-the-2025-developer-survey-results-are-here/)

**Stack Overflow Developer Survey 2024**
- [DATA] Technical documentation (84%) and Stack Overflow (80%) are the top online resources developers use to learn. 90% of documentation users rely on docs that ship in API/SDK packages. The 2024 survey also had sections titled "Influence on technology purchases" and "Researching new tools and technologies", but I could not retrieve their percentages. — [SO 2024 Survey](https://survey.stackoverflow.co/2024/); [SO 2024 blog](https://stackoverflow.blog/2024/08/06/2024-developer-survey/)

**JetBrains State of Developer Ecosystem 2025 (n = 24,534 developers, 194 countries, fielded April–June 2025, published October 2025)**
- [DATA] 85% of developers regularly use AI tools, and 62% rely on at least one AI coding assistant. 68% expect AI proficiency to become a job requirement. — [JetBrains blog](https://blog.jetbrains.com/research/2025/10/state-of-developer-ecosystem-2025/); [InfoWorld](https://www.infoworld.com/article/4077352/85-of-developers-use-ai-regularly-jetbrains-survey.html); [Methodology](https://lp.jetbrains.com/developer-ecosystem-2025-methedology/)
- [DATA] The Register notes JetBrains made CLion, DataGrip, Rider, RubyMine, RustRover and WebStorm free for non-commercial use, while commercial licences remain "pricey". — [The Register, 2025-10-21](https://www.theregister.com/2025/10/21/massive_jetbrains_dev_survey/)

**GitHub Octoverse 2025 (published 2025-10-28)**
- [DATA] More than 180M developers are on GitHub, and more than 36M joined in the past year (about one per second). India added more than 5M developers (over 14% of new accounts), and GitHub projects India will supply one in three new developers globally by 2030. About 80% of new developers use Copilot in their first week. GitHub links the late-2024 launch of **Copilot Free** to a step-change in sign-ups above projections, though it describes the timing as correlation rather than proven cause. — [GitHub Octoverse 2025](https://github.blog/news-insights/octoverse/octoverse-a-new-developer-joins-github-every-second-as-ai-leads-typescript-to-1/)

**SlashData**
- [DATA] Global developer population is about 47.2M at the start of 2025 (46.05M in Q1 2025). — [SlashData population](https://www.slashdata.co/post/global-developer-population-trends-2025-how-many-developers-are-there); [SlashData on X](https://x.com/SlashDataHQ/status/1907814779504222385)
- [DATA] In a Q3 2025 study of about 12,000 developers, ChatGPT (64%) and GitHub Copilot (49%) lead AI tool use. Privacy and quality concerns are slowing wider GenAI rollout. — [SlashData blog](https://www.slashdata.co/blog)
- [DATA, old] SlashData Q3 2017: more than 87% of developers with any leadership function, and two-thirds of front-line coders, are involved in purchasing decisions. Team leads are the biggest influencers at 68%. This figure is old but often cited. — [DevOps.com on SlashData](https://devops.com/slashdata-report-developers-influence-tool-selection/)
- [DATA] SlashData published a December 2024 piece on how technology leaders take part in tool-buying decisions by role. I found only its summary. — [SlashData tech-market research](https://www.slashdata.co/blog/categories/tech-market-research)

### Inferences
- Pricing is the #2 reason developers reject a tool, and cost is near-top for personal projects. For an individual-pay productivity tool, price is therefore an active filter, not a footnote. It becomes a smaller factor once the employer pays.
- AI integration ranks near the bottom both as a reason to adopt and as a reason to reject, and trust in AI accuracy is falling. Leading with "AI-powered" probably adds little persuasive value and may cost credibility. Leading with quality, a clean API or UX, and privacy addresses the top-ranked factors.
- Security and privacy rank as the #1 deal-breaker. Local-first storage, opt-in telemetry and no forced login therefore count as conversion features, not compliance chores (see sections 5 and 7).
- India and other emerging markets supply a growing share of new developers, which strengthens the case for PPP pricing (section 3).

### Gaps
- I could not retrieve SO 2024/2025 breakdowns for *how* developers research tools before buying (peers vs. docs vs. ads vs. reviews). The section exists on survey.stackoverflow.co but the domain was unreachable. The SO 2026 survey results had not been published by 2025-09-30 (SO blog "Getting ready for 2026 results"), and I found no 2026 figures.
- I could not retrieve JetBrains 2025 data on who pays for IDE or AI licences (self vs. employer).
- No current (2024–2026) SlashData figure on purchase influence or on trust in ads vs. peers was found.
- No reliable survey quantifies developers' trust in ads vs. peer recommendations. The claim that peers and docs beat ads is widely asserted but, in what I found, supported only indirectly by the 84% documentation-usage figure and by company anecdotes such as PostHog's.

## 2. Why developers pay for tools despite free options

### Takeaway
The paid-despite-free tools in this research share four traits. They keep a genuinely useful free tier or a one-time licence. They charge for things free alternatives do badly: polish and UX, sync and cloud, team features, and AI compute that has a real marginal cost. They split personal from business pricing so that employers pay more. And they avoid pricing that feels like lock-in.

### Cited Findings
- [DATA] **Raycast**: a permanent free tier covers the launcher, clipboard history, snippets, window management and the full extension store. Pro costs $10/month or $96/year and adds AI, cloud sync and custom themes. Students get 50% off Pro by form; the discount excludes the Advanced AI add-on. — [Raycast pricing](https://www.raycast.com/pricing); [Raycast Pro](https://www.raycast.com/pro)
- [DATA] **Sublime Text**: a perpetual personal licence (widely reported at $99) includes 3 years of updates. Personal upgrades then cost $80, or $152 for the Text + Merge bundle. Businesses *must* buy an annual subscription, from $65/seat/year for the first 10 seats. In short: a one-time price for individuals and a subscription for companies. — [Sublime HQ upgrade page](https://www.sublimehq.com/store/upgrade)
- [DATA] **JetBrains**: WebStorm and Rider became free for non-commercial use in October 2024, later extended to CLion, DataGrip, RubyMine and RustRover. Non-commercial users **cannot opt out** of anonymous usage statistics. Commercial subscribers control telemetry, with detailed collection off by default. OSS, student and classroom licences collect analytics only with explicit consent. — [JetBrains blog 2024-10-24](https://blog.jetbrains.com/blog/2024/10/24/webstorm-and-rider-are-now-free-for-non-commercial-use/); [Non-commercial FAQ](https://sales.jetbrains.com/hc/articles/18950890312210-The-free-non-commercial-licensing-FAQ); [The Register](https://www.theregister.com/2025/10/21/massive_jetbrains_dev_survey/)
- [DATA] **Docker Desktop** is free for personal use, education, open source and businesses under 250 employees *and* under $10M revenue. Professional use above those thresholds needs a paid subscription. This separates "developer as user" from "company as payer". — [TechRepublic](https://www.techrepublic.com/article/docker-launches-new-business-plan-with-changes-to-the-docker-desktop-license/); [Softline](https://www.softline.de/en/about-us/news/docker-desktop-is-no-longer-free-for-enterprise-users/)
- [EVENT] **Cursor** (AI usage costs): in June 2025 Pro moved from request-based limits to a dollar-denominated usage pool, $20 of non-Auto model usage per month plus "unlimited" Auto. Paying for AI tools is increasingly paying for compute with a real marginal cost, which differs from paying for software polish. — [Cursor forum: Clarifying Cursor Pro pricing](https://forum.cursor.com/t/clarifying-cursor-pro-pricing/113803)
- [DATA] SO 2025 found developers endorse tools for quality first and API or ease of use second (section 1). This is consistent with polish and UX being what developers will pay for. — [SO 2025 Work](https://survey.stackoverflow.co/2025/work)

### Inferences
- Common revenue triggers in the cases above are sync or cloud (Raycast Pro), team or business use (Sublime business subscription, Docker's 250-employee threshold, JetBrains commercial licence), AI compute (Raycast AI, Cursor) and update entitlements (Sublime's 3-year window). Core single-user, offline functionality usually stays free or one-time.
- "Expense it to the employer" is built into pricing through the personal/business split. Individuals see a low or one-time price; firms are required to pay a subscription. This lets a developer self-pay small amounts while the company carries the main revenue.
- Free non-commercial tiers (JetBrains) are a funnel: the same developer later needs a commercial licence at work. The free tier is paid for with telemetry, which is a trust trade-off (section 5).

### Gaps
- I did not retrieve TablePlus, Tower, Kaleidoscope, 1Password or Setapp pricing or business-model data (searches returned nothing usable). Treat any claims about them as unverified.
- I found no survey directly measuring the share of developers who pay out of pocket vs. employer-paid for individual productivity tools.

## 3. Pricing tactics that work for developer tools

### Takeaway
The evidence-backed patterns are: free forever for individual or non-commercial use, with paid business or team tiers (Docker, JetBrains, Sublime, Raycast); separate perpetual or one-time licences for individuals vs. subscriptions for businesses (Sublime); student and OSS discounts; transparent, usage-based pricing on raw metrics (PostHog). Freemium dev tools typically convert 1–5% of free users to paid, while trials convert far higher. Usage-based pricing for AI is the riskiest pattern because of perceived "rug pulls".

### Cited Findings
- [DATA] Freemium-to-paid benchmarks vary widely and are mostly vendor-sourced. The median B2B SaaS freemium rate is 2–5%; dev tools are often 1–3% (attributed to OpenView's 2023 benchmarks), and another guide says dev tools are 2–4%. First Page Sage 2026 reports about 3.4% (traditional freemium) and 3.7% (land-and-expand). ChartMogul reports an 8% median free-to-paid rate across products but says few products actually hit 8%. Trials convert much higher: one source gives a 24% median for dev-tool trials, another 17%. — [Monetizely](https://www.getmonetizely.com/articles/whats-the-right-ratio-of-free-to-paid-users-in-developer-saas); [First Page Sage](https://firstpagesage.com/seo-blog/saas-freemium-conversion-rates/); [ChartMogul](https://chartmogul.com/reports/saas-conversion-report/); [1Capture](https://www.1capture.io/blog/free-trial-conversion-benchmarks-2025)
- [DATA] Student pricing: Raycast gives 50% off Pro. JetBrains has free OSS, student and classroom licences, plus free non-commercial licences for several IDEs. — [Raycast pricing](https://www.raycast.com/pricing); [JetBrains data notice](https://www.jetbrains.com/legal/docs/terms/product_data_collection/1.4/)
- [DATA] One-time vs. subscription: Sublime uses a perpetual personal licence with 3 years of updates and paid upgrades ($80), plus a mandatory business subscription ($65/seat/year). — [Sublime HQ](https://www.sublimehq.com/store/upgrade)
- [OPINION/DATA] PostHog runs usage-based pricing on raw inputs such as events and API requests rather than monthly tracked users, which one analysis says makes cost more predictable for engineers. Its pricing is "completely transparent, without needing to talk to sales". — [Schematic podcast on PostHog pricing](https://schematichq.com/podcast/posthogs-pricing-page-pricing-as-an-extension-of-brand); [Why PostHog](https://archive.posthog.com/why)
- [OPINION] PPP pricing: vendor and explainer sources recommend country-tiered prices from World Bank ICP or the Big Mac Index, detected by IP and implemented with multiple Stripe prices. One illustrative calculation turns a $29/month plan into about $7 for India, whose PPP multiplier is roughly 0.3 of the US. These are recommendations and math, not measured outcomes. — [Rocket.new PPP guide](https://www.rocket.new/blog/how-to-add-purchasing-power-parity-pricing-to-your-saas); [PricePush glossary](https://pricepush.app/glossary/purchasing-power-parity); [PPP use cases](https://www.mintlify.com/sachithrrra/ppp/guides/use-cases)
- [EVENT] Usage-based AI pricing risk: Cursor's June 2025 shift to a dollar-based usage pool for Pro led to backlash and refunds (section 5). — [Cursor forum](https://forum.cursor.com/t/clarifying-cursor-pro-pricing/113803)

### Inferences
- For an individual productivity tool, a defensible default is: free core for individuals; a low-priced Pro tier for sync, AI and power features; a higher-priced team or business tier that employers must buy; plus student, OSS and PPP discounts. Each element above is used by at least one tool cited here.
- Trials convert far better than freemium (roughly 17–24% vs. 2–5% on the benchmarks above). This suggests reverse trials (full Pro features for a period, then fall back to free) are worth testing. However, no reverse-trial data for dev tools was found.
- A one-time licence for individuals is attractive to subscription-fatigued developers and can coexist with a business subscription (Sublime model).

### Gaps
- I found no measured before/after results of PPP pricing for a dev tool in India or other emerging markets. Indie Hackers posts with real numbers were not surfaced.
- I found no data on lifetime deals (AppSumo-style) for dev tools: neither outcomes nor their effect on brand.
- No reverse-trial case data for dev tools was found, and Kyle Poyar / OpenView reverse-trial material was not retrieved.
- I found no example of a dev-tool pricing page that ships an "expense this / ask your manager" email template. The closest is a non-dev newsletter's expense-approval template: [RadianceFields](https://radiancefields.com/newsletter/expense_template). The tactic is plausible, but I found no evidence on how well it works.

## 4. Messaging and positioning

### Takeaway
Practitioner consensus, which is opinion rather than measured data, is: educate rather than promote; be transparent (pricing, handbook, code); write plainly with no buzzwords and no "code-washing"; and let product quality and craft (changelogs, docs) carry the brand. PostHog is the best-documented example and attributes about 70% of its growth to recommendations.

### Cited Findings
- [OPINION] Adam DuVander (*Developer Marketing Does Not Exist*): "To reach more developers requires more education and less promotion. Your 'marketing' should not feel like marketing." Typical promotional tactics fall flat, so you should educate and inspire instead. The title's Matrix "there is no spoon" framing means developer marketing exists only insofar as your approach makes it visible. — [Developer Marketing podcast transcript](https://devmarketing.transistor.fm/episodes/how-to-reach-more-developers/transcript); [Nordic APIs review](https://nordicapis.com/book-review-developer-marketing-does-not-exist/); [SlashData interview](https://www.slashdata.co/post/developer-marketing-does-not-exist-with-adam-duvander)
- [OPINION, secondary] A third-party writer credits DuVander with a "DEV" content framework: Discovery, Education, Viewpoint. This is unconfirmed against the book. — (secondary; see [Nordic APIs review](https://nordicapis.com/book-review-developer-marketing-does-not-exist/))
- [OPINION] PostHog's handbook has a "no sneaky shit" principle: no sleazy marketing or sales tactics, usage-based pricing, open-source code. It says not to treat customers as more gullible or more susceptible to marketing than yourself. — [PostHog brand/message handbook](https://posthog.com/handbook/content/brand-message); [PostHog pricing advice](https://posthog.com/newsletter/pricing-advice)
- [DATA/claim] PostHog does no outbound sales, and its pricing is fully public. A growth analysis attributes about 70% of growth to recommendations and 30% to content, and describes the strategy as a narrow ICP (product engineers at high-growth startups), a public handbook, and each feature sold as its own product. — [Why PostHog](https://archive.posthog.com/why); [How PostHog grows (Howtheygrow, mirror)](https://onepage-research.sliplane.app/articles/howtheygrow.co/how-posthog-grows-the-power-of-being-open-core)
- [OPINION] PostHog's pricing page is described as "fun" and closer to shopping online than evaluating enterprise software, with playful CTAs and product "images". Pricing is treated as an extension of the brand. — [Schematic podcast](https://schematichq.com/podcast/posthogs-pricing-page-pricing-as-an-extension-of-brand)
- [OPINION] Developers are "allergic to buzzwords", skeptical of hype and value practicality. "Tone-deaf copy" is the "greatest offender", and "code-washing" (marketing dressed as code) feels try-hard. Developers dislike marketers pretending to "get" lingo or overdoing humour. — [Jen Wike Huger](https://jenwikehuger.substack.com/p/10-things-ive-learned-about-developer); [Ceci Stallsmith / Calyx](https://calyx.substack.com/p/how-to-do-developer-marketing-that); [MarTech](https://martech.org/4-things-developers-really-wish-every-marketer-understood)
- [OPINION] Heavybit (Nick Moore) argues that "developers hate marketing" is a misleading cliché. Developers hate marketing that does not respect their needs. — [Heavybit](https://www.heavybit.com/library/article/developer-marketing-mistakes); [MarTech](https://martech.org/4-things-developers-really-wish-every-marketer-understood)
- [OPINION] Linear's changelog is cited as among the best in the industry: concise, visual, focused on what changed for the user, with technical detail present but secondary. — [ProductLift changelog examples](https://www.productlift.dev/blog/best-changelog-examples/)
- [OPINION] Markepear-related lesson (secondary recap): build trust with champions, because the adopting developer sways the buying decision, and use product-adoption signals to help technical users argue for the tool internally. — [Antoine Buteau: lessons from developer markepear](https://www.antoinebuteau.com/lessons-from-developer-markepear/)

### Inferences
- In the SO 2025 data, developers prize quality and ease of use and rank AI integration near last. This supports the "show, don't tell" approach: demos, docs, honest comparisons and benchmarks address those factors, while adjective-heavy or AI-hype copy does not.
- Transparency in pricing, roadmap, telemetry and code works both as positioning and as a defence against the #1 deal-breaker (security and privacy).

### Gaps
- Direct Markepear (Jakub Czakon) primary content was not retrieved; only a secondary recap was found.
- No primary source was retrieved on Linear's or 37signals' marketing philosophy. Claims about their "anti-marketing" approach are not sourced here.
- No A/B or measured data was found on honest comparison pages or benchmark-led copy vs. conventional copy.

## 5. What fails or backfires

### Takeaway
The biggest documented backfires are trust breaches. Examples include pricing changes communicated unclearly (Cursor 2025), licence relicensing from OSS (HashiCorp 2023 leading to OpenTofu; Redis 2024 leading to Valkey, then a partial reversal to AGPL in 2025), forced accounts or cloud (Warp's login requirement, reversed November 2024; Postman's Scratch Pad removal in 2023, which drove users to Bruno), and non-optional telemetry. In each documented case developers respond with forks, migrations to free alternatives, or public pressure that forces a reversal.

### Cited Findings
- [EVENT] **Cursor (June–July 2025)**: Pro moved to a $20 dollar-denominated usage pool on 2025-06-16. Users said "unlimited" was misleading because it applied to Auto only, and they lacked a real-time usage meter. Cursor acknowledged the changes "were not communicated clearly" and offered full refunds for unexpected charges between June 16 and July 4. CEO Michael Truell's July 4 post, "Clarifying our pricing", took responsibility. — [Cursor forum (primary)](https://forum.cursor.com/t/clarifying-cursor-pro-pricing/113803); [FintechWeekly](https://www.fintechweekly.com/magazine/articles/cursor-pricing-change-user-backlash-refund); [WeAreFounders timeline](https://www.wearefounders.uk/cursors-pricing-disaster-the-full-timeline-of-how-an-ai-coding-darling-burned-its-most-loyal-users/)
- [EVENT] **Warp (November 2024)**: Warp dropped its mandatory login after "hundreds of developers" objected (founder Zach Lloyd). Logged-out users get core features plus a preview of AI. Warp said some features may be gated to logged-in users later. — [Warp blog: Lifting the login requirement](https://www.warp.dev/blog/lifting-login-requirement); [It's FOSS](https://itsfoss.com/news/warp-terminal-no-login/); [OMG! Ubuntu](https://www.omgubuntu.co.uk/2024/11/warp-ai-terminal-login-change)
- [EVENT] **HashiCorp (2023)**: Terraform and Vagrant moved from MPL-2.0 to the Business Source License, which led to the OpenTofu fork. HashiCorp's CEO called the fork "tragic for open source innovation". — [Wikipedia: formerly open-source software](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_formerly_open-source_or_free_software)
- [EVENT] **Redis (2024–2025)**: Redis 7.4 moved from BSD-3 to RSALv2/SSPLv1, announced 2024-03-20. The Linux Foundation launched the Valkey fork from Redis 7.2.4 on 2024-03-28. Redis 8.0 (May 2025) added AGPLv3 as an option. AWS ElastiCache and Google Memorystore reportedly default new clusters to Valkey, and many enterprises ban AGPL dependencies outright, which keeps them on Valkey. — [The Stack: Redis reverts to open source](https://www.thestack.technology/redis-reverts-to-open-source/); [Redis (Wikipedia)](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Redis); [Redis docker-library issue #396](https://github.com/redis/docker-library-redis/issues/396); [BetterDB](https://www.betterdb.com/blog/why-migrate-to-valkey-if-redis-is-open-source-again) (the cloud-default and enterprise-policy claims are secondary)
- [EVENT] **Postman (May 2023 onward)**: Postman phased out the offline Scratch Pad, so collections require an account and cloud sync. Developers objected ("only wanted a local way to manage APIs") and moved to Bruno (MIT, offline-only, collections as plain text in Git), Insomnia and Hoppscotch. Sources disagree on exact timing and on current Insomnia login requirements. — [EchoAPI / Indie Hackers post](https://www.indiehackers.com/post/postman-deprecates-scratchpad-a-developers-journey-to-echoapi-900c3e76ca); [TestMu: Postman vs Insomnia vs Bruno](https://www.testmuai.com/blog/postman-vs-insomnia-vs-bruno/); [StackSheriff alternatives](https://stacksheriff.com/tools/postman-alternatives/)
- [EVENT] **Docker Desktop (2021)**: Docker made a paid subscription mandatory for companies with 250 or more employees or $10M or more in revenue. Coverage frames it as Docker Desktop "no longer free for enterprise users", and alternatives such as Podman are a recurring comparison. — [TechRepublic](https://www.techrepublic.com/article/docker-launches-new-business-plan-with-changes-to-the-docker-desktop-license/); [DevsOperative](https://www.devsoperative.com/blogs/docker-desktop-changes)
- [DATA] **Telemetry**: JetBrains' free non-commercial licence makes anonymous usage statistics non-optional, unlike its paid licences. This shows telemetry used as the price of free. — [JetBrains blog](https://blog.jetbrains.com/blog/2024/10/24/webstorm-and-rider-are-now-free-for-non-commercial-use/)
- [OPINION/EVENT] **Astroturfing**: an older SD Times piece covers Samsung astroturfing on Stack Overflow and Microsoft allegedly astroturfing Xbox posts on Reddit, and notes the resulting outrage. 2026-era allegations against a YC startup (RevisionDojo) running Reddit astroturfing campaigns come from a secondhand source and are unverified. — [SD Times](https://sdtimes.com/astroturfing/sd-times-blog-samsung-responds-to-stackoverflow-astroturfing-another-allegation-in-a-pervasive-corporate-trend); [Feedbagel (unverified)](https://feedbagel.com/post/allegations-of-revisiondojo-a-yc-startup-running-astroturfing-campaigns-targetin)
- [DATA] **Overclaiming AI**: 46% of developers distrust AI accuracy and only 3% highly trust it (SO 2025). AI integration ranks 9th of the endorsement factors. — [SO 2025 press release](https://stackoverflow.co/company/press/archive/stack-overflow-2025-developer-survey/)
- [OPINION] Tone-deaf copy, code-washing and forced lingo or humour backfire (section 4). — [Calyx](https://calyx.substack.com/p/how-to-do-developer-marketing-that); [MarTech](https://martech.org/4-things-developers-really-wish-every-marketer-understood)

### Inferences
- The common pattern is that developers punish *changes to the deal* (licence, price, login, offline) more than the price itself. When a credible free or OSS alternative exists (OpenTofu, Valkey, Bruno), the switching threat is real and shows up in actual migrations.
- Ways to reduce the risk: grandfather existing users, give advance notice, provide usage meters, never retroactively gate offline or local features, and avoid ambiguous words like "unlimited".

### Gaps
- No quantitative data was found on churn or migration caused by these events, such as OpenTofu adoption numbers or Cursor cancellations. Secondary blogs that claim a Valkey "won the market" are opinion.
- No primary documentation was found on gated-content (whitepaper form) performance or aggressive SDR outreach with developers. Both are commonly asserted to backfire but are unsourced here.
- No documented developer-tool fake-review scandal was found.

## 6. Frameworks: developer marketing, PLG to sales-led, developer to champion to buyer

### Takeaway
The working model is bottom-up. Individual developers adopt for free. In-product signals mark product-qualified leads (PQLs): inviting teammates, hitting usage limits, many users from the same company domain. Sales or upgrade prompts then target the champion, who must translate value into ROI and risk terms for the economic buyer (director-level or above, the engineering manager or CTO). Outbound and demand-gen generally do not pay off at small ACVs.

### Cited Findings
- [OPINION] A PQL is a user or account that has gotten real value from the free or trial product and shows buying intent through in-product behaviour, such as inviting a teammate or hitting a usage limit. Unlike an MQL, a PQL has already used the product. — [Trellus](https://www.trellus.ai/learning-center/product-qualified-lead-pql); [Common Room: PQL signals](https://commonroom.io/blog/5-signals-to-help-you-prioritize-your-pqls)
- [OPINION] Bottom-up SaaS companies with small ACVs rely on product qualification because outbound prospecting and demand-gen are not economically viable at those price points. — [Tomasz Tunguz](https://tomtunguz.com/why-startups-should-establish-qualifying-signals-for-sales-teams-early-on/)
- [OPINION] Airtable's product leader observes that accounts which grew on their own sit at a low seniority level, and existing users have no incentive to introduce the vendor to their boss. The suggested fix is to pursue a team-wide deal with the executive most likely to become a champion. If a PQL has a director-level or higher title, they likely hold budget. Users care about functionality, while economic buyers care about ROI and risk. — [Product-Led Sales: PQLs/PQAs](https://productledsales.substack.com/p/pqls-pqas-elegantly-going-around); [Priya Bhatia: Product-Led Sales](https://priyabhatia.substack.com/p/okay-so-you-want-to-do-product-led)
- [DATA, old] Team leads are the largest purchase influencers (68%), according to SlashData 2017. — [DevOps.com](https://devops.com/slashdata-report-developers-influence-tool-selection/)
- [OPINION] Markepear (secondary recap): win champions and give technical users adoption signals so they can argue internally. — [Antoine Buteau](https://www.antoinebuteau.com/lessons-from-developer-markepear/)
- [OPINION] DuVander: education over promotion (section 4). — [Nordic APIs](https://nordicapis.com/book-review-developer-marketing-does-not-exist/)
- [OPINION] Vendor-sourced PQL conversion claims (about 8x MQL conversion; 20–30% PQL-to-close) are not independently sourced, so treat them as indicative only. — [search-surfaced vendor glossary, e.g. Komo](https://komo.ai/glossary/product-qualified-lead)

### Inferences
- For individual productivity tools, the champion-to-buyer path likely runs: an individual pays or uses the free tier → several users appear at the same company domain → a team tier is offered with admin, billing and SSO → the engineering manager or director approves. A one-page "business case" in ROI and risk terms (security posture, data handling, time saved) is the artefact the champion needs.
- Because individual-to-team expansion is the economic engine, team features (shared config, central billing, licence management, SSO) are what justify the higher price an employer pays.

### Gaps
- No developer-tool-specific data was found on how engineering managers or CTOs evaluate productivity tools, or on the share of individual-tool revenue that converts to team plans.
- DevRel models (for example, Heavybit's DevRel frameworks) were not retrieved.

## 7. Selling "productivity": time-saved claims, DORA/SPACE, DX, and what proof developers accept

### Takeaway
"Productivity" claims face an evidence problem. METR's 2025 randomized trial found experienced developers were **19% slower** with AI tools while *believing* they were 20% faster. DORA 2024–2025 links AI adoption to individual gains but worse delivery stability. Self-reported time-saved claims are therefore weak proof to skeptical developers and engineering leaders. Measured, team-level metrics (DORA) and reproducible demos or benchmarks carry more weight.

### Cited Findings
- [DATA] **METR RCT (July 2025)**: 16 experienced OSS developers took 19% *longer* with AI tools (CI +2% to +39%). They had predicted a 24% speedup and afterwards estimated a 20% speedup. METR called it "a snapshot of early-2025 AI capabilities in one relevant setting" and flagged possible selection bias. — [METR (via Harvard tagteam mirror)](https://tagteam.harvard.edu/hub_feeds/3997/feed_items/14760086/about); [DX blog on METR](https://getdx.com/blog/metr-study-on-how-ai-affects-developer-productivity/)
- [DATA, secondary] **METR follow-up (February 2026)**: estimated -18% (CI -38% to +9%) for returning developers and -4% (CI -15% to +9%) for newly recruited developers, characterised as "some evidence for speedup". The sign convention is reported secondhand and should be verified with METR. — [philippdubach.com](https://philippdubach.com/posts/93-of-developers-use-ai-coding-tools.-productivity-hasnt-moved.)
- [DATA, secondary] **DORA 2024**: every 25% increase in AI adoption was associated with about a 1.5% drop in throughput and a 7.2% drop in delivery stability. **DORA 2025**: AI adoption at about 90%. Individual output gains shrink at the team and organisation level, and AI stays negatively correlated with delivery stability, while throughput reportedly turned positive. — [Unblocked: DORA metrics in the AI era](https://getunblocked.com/blog/dora-metrics-ai-era/); [Diginomica](https://diginomica.com/node/29061)
- [DATA] SO 2025: 66% are frustrated by AI output that is "almost right", and 45% say debugging AI code takes significant time. — [SO 2025 press release](https://stackoverflow.co/company/press/archive/stack-overflow-2025-developer-survey/)
- [DATA] JetBrains 2025 says it covers "new productivity metrics", per the report title. Specific findings on which metrics developers prefer were not retrieved. — [JetBrains 2025 report](https://devecosystem-2025.jetbrains.com/)

### Inferences
- Developers and their managers have now seen evidence that self-reported AI productivity can be wrong by about 40 percentage points (METR's perception gap). "Saves X hours per week" claims based on user self-reports are therefore likely to be discounted. Proof that is more likely to be accepted includes: reproducible benchmarks (latency, startup time, keystrokes), live demos or interactive docs, a free tier the developer can test on their own codebase, team-level before/after metrics on DORA- or SPACE-style dimensions, and honest scoping of claims.
- For individual tools such as launchers, editors and Git clients, "productivity" is better sold as reduced friction and flow (speed, polish, fewer context switches), which developers can feel in a free trial, than as quantified hours saved.

### Gaps
- The DORA 2025 report and the SPACE framework paper were not retrieved directly; DORA figures above are from secondary commentary.
- No survey was found on which productivity proof (benchmarks vs. testimonials vs. case studies) developers find most credible.
- JetBrains 2025 productivity-metric findings were not retrieved (domain unreachable).
